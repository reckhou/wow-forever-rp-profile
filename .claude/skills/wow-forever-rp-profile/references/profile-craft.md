# Profile craft: writing an RP profile people want to walk up to

**Abbreviations:** RP = roleplay; IC / OOC = in character / out of character; TRP3 = Total RP 3; "god-moding" = writing other players' characters' thoughts, feelings or actions for them; "Mary Sue" = a character so special, flawless or central that there's nothing left for anyone else to do.

## 1. What a profile is for

A profile is a **menu for other players**, not a biography. Someone hovering over the character in Goldshire or at a Forever campfire has a few seconds of attention. The profile's job is to give them something to *react to*: a visible detail, a rumour, a need.

Keep three layers apart:

| Layer | Who knows it | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Visible | Anyone who looks | Physical description, At first glance, Currently |
| Known | People who've heard of the character | History (public parts), rumours |
| Private | Only the player | The "For the player" notes, never pasted |

Most weak profiles blur these, for example a description that tells the reader the character's secret trauma. Strong ones show the *surface* and let the depths leak through it.

## 2. Physical description

- **Only what an observer can perceive:** build, face, clothing, gear, posture, gait, voice, smell, habits. Third person, present tense.
- **Show the inference, not the fact.** "Holds himself like a man used to a shield on his left arm" invites questions. "He is a veteran of the Second War" answers them.
- **Three or four distinctive details beat a catalogue.** Skip the obvious ("she has two arms"). Pick what makes *this* person memorable: a mended tabard with the insignia unpicked, ink-stained fingers, one hippogryph feather dyed black.
- **Never dictate reactions.** "Everyone finds her captivating" and "You feel uneasy under his gaze" are god-moding. Write "Her gaze lingers a beat too long" and let the reader decide how their character feels.
- **Engage more than sight.** A smell, a sound or a tic is often the hook that sticks.
- **Match the race's real look in Forever.** The game model is what others see. A description can add to it, but can't contradict it: a gnome isn't "towering".

## 3. Personality

- Describe how the character **behaves around others**: the tells a stranger picks up within one conversation.
- Pair every virtue with a **flaw or cost**. Brave becomes reckless; loyal becomes unable to admit the cause is wrong.
- Give them a **want** (what they're chasing) and a **fear** (what they avoid). Those drive scenes.
- One **contradiction** makes a character feel real: the gentle priest with a gambling habit.
- Trait sliders should agree with the prose. If the text says "honest to a fault", the Truthful–Deceitful slider sits far towards Truthful.

## 4. History, rumours and secrets

- **Write the public version.** Keep it to what an acquaintance, a guild-mate or a town gossip could know. Put the real truth in the "For the player" notes.
- **Anchor it to the timeline.** Check each "they were there when…" against the age arithmetic in `forever-lore.md`.
- **Leave gaps.** A missing year, an unexplained scar, a name they won't say. Gaps are where other players' stories go.
- **End with a "Rumours" block** (a TRP3 community convention): 3–6 short rumours in the voice of hearsay. Make some true, some false and some half-true. Record which is which only in the player notes. Rumours let other characters approach with something to say.
- **Lore figures in the background only.** "Served in a company Bolvar Fordragon once inspected" is fine; "Bolvar's sworn brother" isn't.

## 5. At first glance, Currently and OOC

**At first glance** (up to 5 slots): each is a title plus one to three sentences of something noticeable *right now*. Good choices are a distinctive item, a smell or sound, a visible injury, an affiliation badge or a habit. These are explicitly open to comment without any introduction, so make each one a conversation starter. Suggest an icon as a search word.

**Currently** (140 characters or fewer): present tense, IC, actionable. It should tell another player *how to start a scene*.
- Good: "Selling charts of the Riverglades at the Gadgetzan inn; pays well for news of Powderfuse Port." (94)
- Weak: "Brooding." (Nothing to grab.)

**OOC** (140 characters or fewer): player-facing. Time zone or usual hours, walk-ups welcome or not, a contact if wanted, and one line of limits if needed.
- Example: "EU evenings. Walk-ups welcome! Happy to plan stories — whisper me. Lore-friendly, low-violence preferred." (105)

Count the characters, spaces included, and show the count.

## 6. Names

- **Avoid lore-family surnames** unless the player insists, and flag them if so: Wrynn, Proudmoore, Windrunner, Stormrage, Whisperwind, Hellscream, Bloodhoof, Bronzebeard, Mekkatorque, Fordring and the like. Other players will assume kinship.
- **Avoid puns, modern words and pop-culture references** on RP realms.
- When asked for names, offer **three options** with a word on each one's feel.
- Rough conventions, built from established lore names:

| Race | Feel | Examples of the pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Human | Medieval European; trade or place surnames | Aldric Hollins, Mara Ashby, Edwin Tallow |
| Dwarf | Scots/Norse flavour; stone, metal and beard compounds; clans | Brogan Flintbrow, Hilde Coppermantle |
| Gnome | Bouncy syllables; tinkering surnames | Tibbi Sprocketwhistle, Fennick Gearsley |
| Night elf | Flowing Darnassian; moon, leaf and night surnames | Ilanthe Moonbreeze, Faelar Duskbough |
| Skyborne | Darnassian first names; wind, sky and storm surnames | Saeleth Galewright, Iyanna Cloudmere |
| Orc | Hard consonants; "of the [clan]" | Grashka of the Frostwolves, Durgash Ironjaw |
| Tauren | Plain first name; nature or totem surnames | Hakan Stormhorn, Taya Mistwalker |
| Troll | Apostrophes and "j"/"z" sounds; Darkspear ties | Ka'jinto, Rasha'zul, Vez'tani |
| Forsaken | The name from life (usually Lordaeronian), or a grim one they chose | Thomas Hale "the Hollow", Lenore Ashgrave |

- Forever lists first and last names as an in-game feature, but its rules weren't published at the time of writing. The TRP3 name is independent of the in-game name either way.

## 7. Power and specialness

Common red flags: a unique bloodline; secret royalty; sole survivor of a famous event; personally killed or befriended a major lore figure; mastery of several opposed powers; unbeatable in a fight; universally desired; a tragic past that excuses everything.

**Don't refuse the concept. Ground it:**
- Turn a fact into a **rumour** ("Some say she was at Stratholme").
- Add a **cost or limit** (the power frightens them; it costs them memory; it fails at the worst times).
- **Shrink the scale** (not *the* hero of Darrowshire, but one of a dozen survivors who still can't talk about it).
- Give **others room**: needs, debts and questions only other characters can resolve.

Forever's tone ("Not every story has to be about saving the world") rewards this. A believable farrier with a debt to the Defias is more playable than a chosen one.

## 8. Revising someone else's profile

- **Keep the player's voice, names and core events.** It's their character. Change what's broken, not what's merely different from how you'd write it.
- Fix in this order: lore errors → god-moding and reactions → hidden information shown in the description → length and tooltip limits → prose polish.
- After the revised profile, list **every substantive change and why** in a short bullet list, so the player can reject any of them.
