# Loch Modan

The waters of Loch Modan lie cradled in gray stone, held by the Stonewrought Dam, a work of dwarven engineering that no other structure on Azeroth has ever equaled. The mountains rise sheer on every side, lightly wooded with pine and birch, and the loch itself sits still and quiet, as though nothing had ever disturbed it.

The loch was a battlefield once. In the Second War the orcs of the Horde swept into the region and made their stand here against the Alliance. Ironforge held. Its dwarves threw the orcs back and reclaimed Loch Modan, and the wreckage of that war was never fully cleared: broken catapults and shattered ballistae still lie scattered across the lakebed and its islands, half-sunk, the only proof left that this calm water was ever a killing ground.

## Algaz Station

Algaz Station stands east of the North Gate Pass, taking its name from Dun Algaz to the north. Here dwarven mountaineers keep their watch, with Mountaineer Stormpike among them, and the armorer Gothor Brumn tends to their gear.

## Dun Algaz

The tunnel through Dun Algaz is older than the quarrel that split the dwarves. When the War of the Three Hammers drove the Wildhammers from Ironforge, it was through this pass that they made their retreat.

It carried the Horde's retreat as well. Beaten back from Dun Modr, the orcs fell south across the Thandol Valley and dug in at the outpost here, and Anduin Lothar, wanting them pushed clear out of Khaz Modan, sent his forces to tear the stronghold down. The assault failed. The orcs held on, and Dun Algaz became the last ground the Horde kept in the region, a foothold that would not be pried loose.

The dwarven mountaineers who watched this pass kept mostly to the hills above it, but the Dragonmaw clan overran their post and drove them back to the upper tunnel, where they hold on still. With the lower pass lost, the road between the dwarven homelands and Menethil Harbor has grown far more dangerous.

## Grizzlepaw Ridge

Grizzlepaw Ridge rises south of Thelsamar, thick with bears that have made the heights their own. Chief among them is Ol' Sooty, a bear of fearsome size and temper, who wanders down from his lair to the base of the valley, quick to pounce upon anyone unfortunate enough to irritate him.

Gorick Guzzledraught keeps to the ridge as well.

## Ironband's Excavation Site

Ironband's Excavation Site, in southeastern Loch Modan, is a dwarven digsite opened by Prospector Ironband in search of what the ancients had buried. But the earth gave up more than relics: troggs, larger and more volatile than those found at the other dwarven digs, rose from below and overran the site, driving off the prospectors and miners who had opened it.

## Mo'grosh Stronghold

The caves of Mo'grosh Stronghold lie in northeastern Loch Modan, home to a tribe of ogres, their darkmaulers and earthbenders answering to no one but their own.

## North Gate Pass

The pass cuts through the high mountains, dropping from Dun Morogh down toward Loch Modan. It is the shortest road between the two, and travelers bound for the lands beyond the Loch pass through here more than any other way.

The North Gate Outpost stands guard here, holding the passage.

## Silver Stream Mine

The Silver Stream Mine once ran deep with silver, and for generations the dwarves of Ironforge drew wealth from its veins. When the ore ran dry, the Miners' League turned the tunnels into a storage depot rather than abandon them.

The mine still holds one thing worth taking: Charged Rift Gems, found nowhere else, prized for the making of mage robes.

But the dwarves no longer hold these tunnels. Tunnel Rat kobolds have overrun the mine, seeking its remaining resources for themselves.

## South Gate Pass

South Gate Pass cuts through the mountains, joining the high snows of Dun Morogh to the lower valleys of Loch Modan. It is the shortest way down to Thelsamar, worn by travelers between the two lands.

Here stands South Gate Outpost, a guard station set to watch the pass.

## Stonesplinter Valley

Stonesplinter Valley cuts among the mountains of southwestern Loch Modan. Once pristine, it now belongs to the Stonesplinter troggs, who have overrun its floor and slopes. The dwarves of Loch Modan count them as vermin to be culled. Deepest in the valley dwells Grawmug, the leader the troggs rally behind.

## Stonewrought Dam

The Stonewrought Dam rises east of Dun Algaz pass, along the northern edge of Loch Modan.

Its builder was a Dark Iron dwarf. Franclorn Forgewright, architect of that clan, devised the stonewrought method by which the dam was raised, though few who cross it know a Dark Iron hand built it.

The dam holds back the Loch, swelling it to more than twice its former size, and the flooding it caused made the wetlands beyond it, ground once mostly underwater.

The Dark Iron clan struck at the dam, and Ironforge answered by tightening its watch. Chief Engineer Hinderweir VII now holds that charge.

## Stoutlager Inn

The Stoutlager Inn stands in Thelsamar, its hearth rarely cold. Dwarves and travelers alike gather here to drink and trade word of the Loch, and few places in the region draw so steady a crowd through the door.

## The Farstrider Lodge

Farstrider Lodge stands in southeastern Loch Modan, its architecture unmistakably high elven, though only one of that race remains within its halls. Humans and dwarves have since taken up residence there, hunters among them, living within walls raised by hands long since departed from this place.

## The Loch

The Loch fills the heart of Loch Modan, the largest body of water on all of Khaz Modan, and third in size across the known world, behind only Lordamere and Darrowmere.

## Thelsamar

Thelsamar sits on the western shore of Loch Modan, north of Grizzlepaw Ridge and northeast of Stonesplinter Valley. A road runs just west of town, climbing through the passes toward Dun Morogh and the Wetlands. Gryphons nest at its roost, and its inn shelters travelers crossing the high country.

## Valley of Kings

The Valley of Kings lies in the southwestern reach of Loch Modan, where the green land narrows toward the gate leading down into the blackened Searing Gorge. That gate stands locked and watched, held under the eye of Mountaineer Pebblebitty, who yields passage only to those who prove themselves worthy of it.

What draws the eye here are the two great statues that rise over the pass: Madoran Bronzebeard and Khardros Wildhammer, carved in stone to stand watch over the road south. When the two kings died, it was their sons who raised the statues together, binding Bronzebeard and Wildhammer in stone as their fathers had been bound in life. The southlands beyond had been turned to fire and ash by Ragnaros, and the statues face that ruin directly: a warning to any who would move against the dwarven kingdoms, and a reminder of the price the Dark Irons paid for what they had done.
