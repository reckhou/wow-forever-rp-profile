# Swamp of Sorrows

Beneath the black water of the swamp lies a temple older than the sorrow that named this place. When the Gurubashi Empire fell, a renegade band of trolls called the Atal'ai fled into the marsh and raised the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, where they practiced grisly rites to summon their dark loa, Hakkar the Soulflayer, back into the world. Their magic twisted the plants and beasts around the temple until the corruption could not go unnoticed. Ysera, mistress of dreams, learned of the cult's ambitions and turned her power against them, drowning the temple beneath the largest bog before Hakkar could return. But she did not trust the trolls to abandon their god forever. She set her consort Eranikus and a host of green dragons to keep eternal watch over the ruin, so it might never again be used to loose such evil upon the world. Green dragonspawn still patrol these marshes at her command, and the sunken temple remains sealed beneath the water, watched rather than destroyed.

## Fallow Sanctuary

In the Swamp of Sorrows stands the Fallow Sanctuary, home to a tribe of Lost Ones known simply as the Lost Ones. Homesickness has taken every one of them, driving them fully feral, so that nothing of who they once were remains to greet anyone who comes near their village.

## Itharius's Cave

In the southwestern Swamp of Sorrows, a cave shelters the high elf Itharius. Outside, unruly green whelps scrap and stumble, too young to master wing or fire. Itharius has taken it upon himself to watch over them as they grow.

## Misty Reed Post

Misty Reed Post is a small outpost of Stonard, set between the Stagalbog and the southern Misty Reed Strand. Tok'Kar holds it, with Katar, a handful of Stonard hunters, and a Stonard shaman. It is only a camp, no burrow or tower, though Tok'Kar dreams of one day raising one.

## Misty Reed Strand

The Misty Reed Strand runs along the Swamp of Sorrows, a gloomy stretch of beach where little grows but driftwood and rot. Crocolisks bask in its shallows, and crabs and murlocs keep to the shore beyond.

## Misty Valley

Mist hangs low over this corner of the Swamp of Sorrows, thick enough to swallow sound. Deep in a cave at the valley's back dwelt the Mire Lord, an elemental force.

## Pool of Tears

The Pool of Tears lies broad and still on the eastern edge of the Swamp of Sorrows. Beneath its calm surface sits the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, sunk there by Ysera after the trolls raised it, hidden from the world. The green dragonflight long kept watch over the drowned place, guarding against anything that might wake what slept below.

Blood-thirsty frenzies move through the water now, and broken Atal'ai stonework litters the shore.

## Sorrowmurk

The Sorrowmurk lies east of the Pool of Tears, near the Sunken Temple. It belongs to dragonkin, green dragonspawn crawling through its mire under the watch of a great green drake, which keeps all but the most reckless travelers away.

## Splinterspear Junction

Where the road into the Swamp of Sorrows forks, an old orcish watch tower still stands, and Horde patrols keep the crossing under their eyes.

The tower is what remains of Kyross, an orcish outpost raised here during the First War. Kyross stood nearer to the human township of Grand Hamlet than any other Horde position in the swamp, and Stormwind could not let it be. War parties were sent to tear it down.

The tower still watches the fork in the road.

## Stagalbog

South of the Pool of Tears, where the Swamp of Sorrows rises into mountains, the marsh thickens into the Stagalbog. West of the wet wilderness, at the mountain's edge, a cave opens into the rock.

## Stagalbog Cave

In the eastern Stagalbog of the Swamp of Sorrows, a cave opens into the mire.

## Stonard

Stonard rises out of the mire of the Swamp of Sorrows, its great hall still standing where so many other orcish halls have long since fallen. Two burrows guard the approach, and orcish patrols move out to hold the small encampments nearby, each one another claim staked against the swamp.

The outpost is old, older than almost any other orcish foothold on this side of the world, sister to the fortress once known as Rockard. It was raised by the Old Horde in the Black Morass in the days after the Dark Portal first opened. When the Second War came and the Horde broke, Stonard fell quiet, likely abandoned or destroyed along with so much else the orcs had built.

It did not stay empty. With the rise of the new Horde and a renewed will to hold this ground, Stonard was rebuilt, its hall restored, its burrows re-dug. Rockard was not so fortunate -- the Dreadmaul ogres took the old fortress for their own and renamed it Dreadmaul Hold, leaving Stonard the lone Horde outpost in the swamp, the last hall of its kind still lit in these lowlands.

## The Harborage

In the northern reaches of the Swamp of Sorrows stands a refuge of those who crossed over from Draenor to Azeroth and, unlike so many of their kin, kept their minds whole. Magtoor leads them here.

## The Shifting Mire

The silt of the Shifting Mire never lies still. It gathers along the ground in fine drifts and creeps across the swamp as though something unseen were dragging it, curling and pooling like a living thing.

Some say the force behind that movement is not malevolent, but some positive energy stirring the silt without any will to harm.

## The Temple of Atal'Hakkar

More than a thousand years ago, the Gurubashi Empire tore itself apart in civil war. At its heart were the Atal'ai, troll priests who sought to bring the blood god Hakkar the Soulflayer back into the world. Defeated and exiled, they fled north into the Swamp of Sorrows and raised a temple there to prepare for his return.

The dragon Aspect Ysera learned of their plan and sank the temple beneath the marsh, meaning to bury their work forever. It did not happen that way. Undisturbed in the drowned dark, shielded from any eye that might have stopped them, the Atal'ai carried on their rituals in peace. The green dragons Ysera set to guard the ruins have kept watch ever since, holding closed a door that was never truly sealed.

Still the temple sits sunken and half-flooded, its altars to Hakkar unbroken, and those who enter risk worse than death. The Loa of Blood is owed souls as well as flesh, and every one it is given feeds the day it does not fail.
