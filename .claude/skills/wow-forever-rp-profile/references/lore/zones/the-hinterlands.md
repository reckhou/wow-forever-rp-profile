# The Hinterlands

After the War of the Three Hammers, the Wildhammer dwarves abandoned their tunnels at Grim Batol and came north, building their homes above ground at Aerie Peak. They turned from stone and forge toward sky and wing, binding themselves to the wild gryphons of the region so closely that they raised a great stone gryphon in their own city to honor the bond. The forest trolls who already held this land, remnants of the once-great Amani Empire, saw the dwarves as squatters on ancestral ground and raided the aeries whenever they could.

When the Horde swept through during the Second War, Thane Kurdran Wildhammer and his riders, until then unaware such invaders even existed, fell on the orc columns from the sky with storm-touched hammers, hounding them without pause. Orgrim Doomhammer hoped to draw the Alliance's strength into the Hinterlands so he might strike Quel'Thalas unopposed, but Anduin Lothar saw the plan and rode to Kurdran's side himself, and together they broke the invaders and drove them back to the Alterac Mountains. For that, Kurdran pledged his people's aid to the Alliance.

## Aerie Peak

The Wildhammer dwarves did not choose the Hinterlands first. Their home was Grim Batol, deep in the mountain, until the War of the Three Hammers ended and the dwarves returned to find their fortress poisoned by the death of Modgud. Grim Batol could not be lived in again. So the Wildhammers built anew here, atop the mountainside rather than within it, unwilling after the horrors of tunnel-fighting against the Dark Iron dwarves to bury themselves in stone again. The Aerie stands open to the sky because of that war.

It was from here that Kurdran Wildhammer led his gryphon riders against orc forces sent to strike the Hinterlands as a feint while the true Horde army moved on Quel'Thalas. Anduin Lothar's forces came to his aid and helped drive the orcs and forest trolls from Wildhammer land, and for that Kurdran pledged his clan to the Alliance.

Falstad Wildhammer, left as High Thane when Kurdran did not return, rules the Aerie still. The forest trolls of the Hinterlands, who call the Wildhammers squatters on ancestral ground, still raid the gryphon aeries, while the high elves of Quel'Danil remain the dwarves' allies against that same enemy.

## Agol'watha

Agol'watha's stones are older than the trolls who last held them sacred, raised as a temple of the Amani Empire before that empire fell. The Vilebranch trolls who came after claimed the ruin for their own. Now the temple has been overrun by animated sludges that fill its shattered courts.

## Bogen's Ledge

Bogen's Ledge is a cave in the southwestern Hinterlands.

## Featherbeard's Hovel

Beside Aerie Peak stands a small house that once belonged to someone called Featherbeard. It holds no garrison, no treasure. It serves no purpose now but to stand where it always stood, empty of whatever life once filled it.

## Hiri'watha

The Witherbark trolls hold Hiri'watha, a village set south of Quel'Danil Lodge in the Hinterlands, deep in the forest they claim as their own.

## Jintha'Alor

Jintha'Alor rises in terraces above the Hinterlands, second in size among the forest troll cities only to Zul'Aman. It was raised in the days of the Amani Empire and takes its name from Jintha, one of the Amani's ancient leaders.

The city belongs to the Vilebranch tribe, who have turned its heights into a fortress of sacrifice. Vile Priestess Hexx rules there in service to Hakkar the Soulflayer, and the Vilebranch prey without mercy on the weaker Revantusk tribe to feed their god's hunger. Elder Torntusk of the Revantusk was taken in one such raid and is held captive within the city still; his guard captain, Huntsman Malkhor, was captured in the same attack and executed, his remains thrown to the wolves and oozes kept within the walls.

The Wildhammer clan and the Revantusk tribe, bound by shared grievance against the Vilebranch, have laid siege to the city together, seeking to end its raids and win vengeance for their dead.

## Plaguemist Ravine

A mist hangs where the mountain wall breaks open west of Quel'Danil Lodge, marking the ravine that cuts north toward Caer Darrow. Nothing lives in the passage. No birds cross it, no beasts den in its rock.

The Hinterlands around it stay green and clean, but the ravine breathes something else, a haze that does not belong to the forest. It is why the place carries the name it does now: Plaguemist, given for the sickly fog that fouls this one path while the rest of the land remains untouched.

## Quel'Danil Lodge

Quel'Danil Lodge is one of the last true gatherings of the high elves, the larger of only two such settlements remaining in Lordaeron. After the Third War it became a place of correspondence, its ambassadors reaching out to the other peoples of the Alliance. That outreach won the Highvale elves an alliance with the Wildhammer dwarves, and it earned them the hatred of the Revantusk trolls, who sought to see the elves slaughtered and their records burned. The documents survived, records of deaths, births, seasons, and every treaty the elves had made.

## Revantusk Village

Revantusk Village stands on the coast of the Hinterlands, the last home of a forest troll tribe that chose loyalty over survival.

When the Old Horde broke in the Second War, the orcs' captors turned on their former allies, and forest troll tribes across the land abandoned the Horde without hesitation. The Revantusk did not. They lacked the numbers to free the captive orcs, who in any case showed little will to be freed, but their continued loyalty marked them for attack by other trolls who wanted nothing more to do with orcs. Driven from their lands, the Revantusk fled to the Hinterlands coast, hoping isolation would keep them safe, and built their village there.

Word eventually reached them that Thrall had remade the Horde and led his people back to the shamanistic ways of their ancestors. The Revantusk sent their fastest runners to find him, and out of that meeting came a pact of friendship between the tribe and the new Horde. Other forest trolls have never forgiven it, calling the Revantusk weak and treacherous, while the Revantusk name all who deserted the Horde as the true traitors.

## Seradane

In the northeastern Hinterlands lies Seradane, where a great circular portal stands open to the Emerald Dream. The forest belongs to the green dragonflight, drawn to guard the threshold their kind holds sacred. The drakes Rothos and Dreamtracker made their home among these trees.

Most who keep the woods are dragonspawn, born of the flight's power. They move in groups of three, a Verdantine Oracle flanked by Boughguards and Tree Warders, patrolling the tangled paths in a standing watch over the way to the Dream.

## Shadra'Alor

In the Hinterlands stands Shadra'Alor, a settlement raised by the forest trolls of the Amani Empire. Here the loa Shadra could be called down and summoned, a place set apart for that purpose alone.

## Shaol'watha

Shaol'watha is a ruin of the Vilebranch forest trolls in the northeastern Hinterlands. It was once a temple.

## Shindigger's Camp

Rhapsody Shindigger, a dwarf fond of drink, keeps his small camp in the Hinterlands alongside the high elf Gilveradin Sunchaser, an odd pairing that holds this quiet stretch of ground.

## Skulk Rock

Skulk Rock is a forest troll ruin overrun with slimes. A cave beneath it is one of the few places where Ghost Mushrooms still grow.

During the Second War the Old Horde held the rock as a base, until the Alliance of Lordaeron drove them out.

## The Altar of Zul

The Altar of Zul rises in the Hinterlands, raised by the Vilebranch forest trolls to their old rites. Qiaga the Keeper stands watch atop the stones, guardian of whatever power the altar still holds.

## The Creeping Ruin

The Creeping Ruin lies broken in the Hinterlands, stone once shaped by forest troll hands. Slimes creep through its fallen halls now, oozing through the old troll stones where the trolls once walked.

## The Overlook Cliffs

The Overlook Cliffs mark the place where the Hinterlands stop being mountains and become sky. The great river that winds through the region gathers itself here and pours over the edge in a single massive waterfall, falling to the shoreline below.

The cliffs are steep and deceptively high. Many who walked to their edge misjudged the drop and fell to their deaths.

## Valorwind Lake

Valorwind Lake lies quiet northeast of Shadra'Alor, its waters seemingly unremarkable. Yet along its edges grows something found nowhere else known in the world: the Violet Tragan, a mysterious mushroom whose secrets remain its own.

## Wildhammer Keep

Wildhammer Keep rises at the heart of Aerie Peak, the seat of the Wildhammer dwarves who hold it. Its halls are open to travelers who reach the peak.

## Zun'watha

The stones of Zun'watha once sheltered, and still shelter, the Witherbark trolls of the Hinterlands. Among the ruins they left standing, a Zul'arek hatefowler is seen from time to time, walking where the Witherbark still dwell.
