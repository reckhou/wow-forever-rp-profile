# Darkshore

A grey mist lies along this coast more often than not, and the night elves have long taken it as a warning. Rain and rock make the land unwelcoming, and every attempt to settle it beyond the trading post of Auberdine has come to nothing.

Ten thousand years past, the twin cities of Ameth'Aran and Bashal'Aran stood here, ruled by Athrikus Narassin. When the War of the Ancients broke the world and the Great Sundering came on, Athrikus swore to his people that he would save them. He kept the letter of his promise and broke its spirit: he bound their souls into soul gems, telling them this was eternal life, while in truth he drained them to feed his own power. The cities fell to ruin, and what walks among the stones there now is not wholly at rest.

After the Third War, Fandral Staghelm brought druids of the Cenarion Circle into Darkshore and planted a new World Tree, Teldrassil, upon this shore.

## Ameth'Aran

The ruins of Ameth'Aran lie southeast of Auberdine in Darkshore, and something of the town still walks there.

Queen Azshara once raised Ameth'Aran as a twin to Bashal'Aran, seating her favored Highborne within its bazaars and its temples to Elune. Over it she placed Athrikus Narassin, a mage of great power who kept to his Tower of Althalaxx.

When the Sundering came and the town was already crumbling, its people clung to the ground as if that alone might save them. Athrikus Narassin appeared among them and promised a spell that would spare them the destruction to come, granting them life eternal within soul gems he carried. As he spoke it, the elves around him died. What eternity he gave them was a prison, not a rescue, and it is their spirits, not their lives, that have lingered in Ameth'Aran ever since.

Two great stone tablets still stand among the ruins, carved with the story of the town's fall. A sentinel who came to read them was set upon by the restless Highborne before she could finish, and fled.

## Auberdine

Auberdine stands where the Sundering broke the land, on a coast that swallowed the great cities of the old Kaldorei Empire. Every settlement attempted in Darkshore since has failed, save this one town on the Veiled Sea.

It has grown unlike the rest of night elven society. Its port draws ships and traders from the Eastern Kingdoms, and its streets are filled with dwarves, gnomes, humans, even high elves, so that Auberdine is lit brighter than a night elf town has reason to be, its signposts written in two tongues for the sake of strangers. Darnassus looks down on it for this. Broll Bearmantle, who once visited the town, considers its people little better than outcasts and misfits, and the Sentinels garrisoned here keep apart from the folk they guard. Yet Shandris Feathermoon, who was once stationed here, remembers it as a place that rang with laughter.

Near the lighthouse, the wreck of a ship once commanded by the elf Elura still marks the rocks, its cargo of elunite ore lost when it went down.

## Bashal'Aran

Before the sea rose over the world and broke it, Queen Azshara raised twin towns in northern Darkshore for her favored Highborne: Ameth'Aran and Bashal'Aran. She set Athrikus Narassin, an elf of great magical power, to govern them, and beneath him served Asterion, a mage as skilled in arcane craft as in arms, who lived among the people of Bashal'Aran and traveled often to Ameth'Aran to tend to its folk. In those days the twin cities held bazaars and temples to Elune, graced by the queen's own favor.

When the first Well of Eternity was shattered, both cities fell to ruin. But at some point Athrikus Narassin turned his craft against his own second, binding Asterion within a moonstone seal and imprisoning him among the stones of Bashal'Aran. Why the master turned on his servant no one now living can say. Asterion endured the prison through a long and painful life, and when at last he died, he found that death had not freed him. His spirit remained bound as surely as his flesh had been.

## Blackwood Den

In the far south of Darkshore, near the border of Ashenvale, a scatter of furbolg camps and a small cave make up the place called Blackwood Den, sometimes named Blackwood Hold. The furbolgs who hold it are corrupted, twisted from whatever they once were.

Within the cave sits a lone night elf named Volcor, held captive among the furbolgs.

## Cliffspring Falls

Cliffspring Falls is the source of the Cliffspring River, once its purest water and the drinking source for all of Darkshore. That water is no longer clean. The cave from which the falls rise has turned foul, and the corruption spreads with the current down through the region it once sustained.

No one agrees why. Some blame Felwood, saying the taint simply seeps outward from that ruined forest. Others point to what has been found deep in the cave itself, where naga now crawl through the dark at the water's source.

## Cliffspring River

The Cliffspring River runs from Cliffspring Falls to the sea in northern Darkshore. Its waters are tainted by the Stormscale naga, and wildlife that drinks from it often falls sick.

## Grove of the Ancients

The Grove of the Ancients is an old night elven temple, its stones settled around the surging Wildbend River in the mountains of Darkshore. Here Onu, an Ancient of Lore, dwells with a sleeping druid and a pair of merchants, and here the Circle of Ancients still gathers.

## Mist's Edge

The coast called Mist's Edge runs the length of Darkshore, the longest shoreline in the region.

It is a gloomy stretch: gray beaches and grim bays, littered with the huge carcasses of dead sea animals and the wrecks of sunken ships.

Crabs scuttle across the whole length of it, and murlocs lurk in the shallow bays, an ever-present threat to anyone walking the shore.

## Remtravel's Excavation

Prospector Remtravel gave his name to this dig site in Darkshore. In his digging he found a fossil unlike any other, humming with mystic properties, but the absent-minded prospector has since lost track of where he put it.

The excavation itself has grown dangerous: Gravelflint troggs have claimed its tunnels, and huge golems, unearthed by the digging, now stir within it.

## Ruins of Mathystra

Past the Tower of Althalaxx, in the north of Darkshore, lie the ruins of Mathystra. Before the Great Sundering, it stood as a city of the night elves, raised in the age of the Highborne. When the Well of Eternity tore itself apart and the world broke beneath it, Mathystra was ruined along with so much else, and the night elves who survived did not return to rebuild it. They withdrew instead to smaller villages along the coast further south, leaving the ruins behind.

Scattered relics of the old Highborne still lie among the fallen stones, and young night elves sometimes come searching through Mathystra for them, wary of the ghost sabers that prowl there among the ruins of what their people once built.

## The Long Wash

The Long Wash runs south from Auberdine to the waters west of Ameth'Aran, the shortest stretch of coast in Darkshore despite its name. Gray sand lies beneath an unbroken overcast sky. The tide leaves little but driftwood and the carcasses of dead sea creatures. Murlocs are seen along the shore.

## The Master's Glaive

In the southwest of Darkshore lies a colossal skull, half-swallowed by earth, with an adamantine glaive still driven through the bone. This was Soggoth the Slitherer, a herald of the Old Gods, and the blade is called the Master's Glaive.

Tablets uncovered at Remtravel's Excavation told what happened here. The titans had sent twenty stone giants against Soggoth, and the battle that followed was so terrible that only one of them lived to see its end: Kronn, the largest of the twenty. As Soggoth's tendrils closed around him, Kronn did not fight free of them. He let himself be dragged inward, closer and closer to the creature's skull, until he was near enough to strike. The blow that killed Soggoth was struck from within its own grasp, and the sword left buried in its skull took the name of that victory.

The Twilight's Hammer has since claimed the site, digging into Soggoth's remains, working to undo what Kronn's sword accomplished and call the Old God's herald back into the world.

## Tower of Althalaxx

In the age of the Great Sundering, when Queen Azshara raised the Highborne above all others, she set an elf named Athrikus Narassin to govern the twin towns of Ameth'Aran and Bashal'Aran. Athrikus served her well enough at first, but the work bored him, and he took to spending his days away from the towns, in a tower of his own called Althalaxx.

Ten thousand years later, what had long been whispered of that tower proved true. A cult calling itself the Cult of the Dark Strand had gathered there, warlocks and a satyr among them, and at their head stood Athrikus Narassin himself. Their purpose was unclear until it came out that the cult answered to the Shadow Council, and that Althalaxx had become one more thread in that old conspiracy's web.

## Twilight Vale

The forests south of Ameth'Aran, running to the border of Ashenvale, are called Twilight Vale. Corrupted furbolgs, feral nightsabers, and rabid bears haunt these woods, and travelers are counseled to keep to the roads rather than wander beneath the trees.

## Wildbend River

The Wildbend River carries its sickness down from the mountains, a continuation of the Bloodvenom River spilling out of Felwood into southern Darkshore, near the Grove of the Ancients. Its waters run tainted, the corruption of Felwood following the current down into these lower lands.
