# Westfall

The wind runs unbroken over Westfall's fields now, for nothing tall remains to break it. Once this was the breadbasket of Stormwind, feeding the young kingdom for generations.

When the Stonemasons Guild went unpaid for rebuilding Stormwind after the Second War, and the guard turned against their protests, Guildmaster Edwin VanCleef led his men into hiding in the countryside rather than face King Varian's justice. There they became the Defias Brotherhood, and beneath a barn in Moonbrook, VanCleef found an old mining network deep enough to hide the building of a weapon meant to burn Stormwind from the sea. He drove off the farmers, hired Riverpaw gnolls to hold the land, and had his men burn the fields and salt the earth so nothing would grow there again.

Stormwind, its coffers spent, left Westfall to fend for itself. Only Gryan Stoutmantle answered, raising the People's Militia at Sentinel Hill when no army came.

The fields he salted have not forgiven Westfall for it. The grass still grows yellow and thin where wheat once stood shoulder-high.

## Alexston Farmstead

The fields at Alexston Farmstead once fed families before the Defias Brotherhood took the land for their own. Now only the mechanical Harvest Watchers move among the furrows, tireless and untended, guarding rows that no one plants anymore.

Baros Alexston, who came to serve Stormwind City as an architect, was born to this soil. He labored here as a boy, among the same fields the Harvest Watchers now patrol, before the land that shaped him passed into other hands.

## Demont's Place

Little remains of Demont's Place but broken timber and overgrown fields, west of Moonbrook. Once a working farmstead, it now lies occupied by Defias agents, who hold it as their own.

## Furlbrow's Pumpkin Farm

Furlbrow's Pumpkin Patch was once tended by Farmer Furlbrow and his wife Verna, but when the Defias Brotherhood swept through Westfall with their mechanical harvest golems, the farm fell like so many others in that country. Furlbrow and Verna fled, taking refuge on the road near the Jansen Stead to the north, leaving their pumpkins and their name behind them.

## Gold Coast Quarry

Gold Coast Quarry once yielded gold and ore for Stormwind, before Westfall fell into ruin. The Defias Brotherhood holds it now, working the pits under their own banner. The People's Militia and its members have clashed with the Brotherhood here, ambushed by Defias among the stone they still patrol.

## Jangolode Mine

The Jangolode Mine once ran ore to Stormwind, one of Westfall's steadier sources of mineral wealth.

That steadiness broke when the Defias Brotherhood seized the mine. Kobolds have since crept into its shafts and claimed them for their own.

## Longshore

The coast of Westfall runs long and empty, one of the widest beaches in Azeroth. Murlocs move along it in numbers too great to hold back, and among them swims Old Murk-Eye, a creature the fishing-folk of the coast have learned to fear and name. Out past the breakers lie the bones of ships that never made it in, elven destroyers among them, given over to the tide.

On the far southwestern reach of the beach stands the farm of Daphne Stilwell. The Defias Brotherhood came for her more than once, meaning to burn her out as they burned so much else in Westfall. Word of her trouble reached Duthorian Rall, who sent aspiring Knights of the Silver Hand down to the coast to stand watch over her land, and they held it.

## Moonbrook

Beneath the fields of Moonbrook once ran a third of Stormwind's treasury, gold pulled from the tunnels later called the Deadmines. The town was the kingdom's breadbasket, until the disbanding of the Stonemasons Guild set off riots that killed Queen Tiffin Wrynn. King Varian's vengeance for her death fell hardest on the guild, and the Stonemasons fled into the farmlands of Westfall to escape him. Stoked from the shadows, their anger became the Defias Brotherhood. Under Edwin VanCleef they turned to banditry, drove the farmers from Moonbrook, and took the abandoned mine tunnels beneath the town as their headquarters, working there in secret to build a weapon meant to destroy Stormwind from the sea.

For years no army has come to answer them. The Brotherhood holds Moonbrook still, and only Gryan Stoutmantle's People's Militia stands against them, gathering strength at Sentinel Hill for a reckoning yet to come.

## Saldean's Farm

Saldean's Farm alone in Westfall was never overrun by the Defias Brotherhood. Defias harvest golems roam its fields, yet Farmer Saldean and his wife Salma refused to leave. Their farmhouse has served for years as a halfway house, the couple doing what they can to keep their guests alive.

## Sentinel Hill

Sentinel Hill stands on high ground above the farmlands of Westfall, its tower watching over cottages, forge, and mill built into the slope below.

Both the Riverpaw gnolls and the Defias Brotherhood have struck at the hill and the lands it holds.

## Stendel's Pond

A small pond lies southwest of Sentinel Hill, its water gone still beneath a ring of gnolls. Two graves rest at its edge, along with scattered old bones, and something in the water seems wrong, as if it were never meant for drinking again.

Fishermen who try their luck there mostly pull up common sagefish. But it is said something else swims beneath the surface, a fish called Stendel's Bane, rarely seen and rarer still caught.

## The Dagger Hills

The Dagger Hills rise as a ridge at the back of Moonbrook, in the south of Westfall. Little stirs there now. Grimbooze Thunderbrew keeps himself among the hills, one of the only living souls to be found in that quiet stretch of ground, and the ghoul Leprithus is known to rise from the earth there as well.

## The Dead Acre

The Dead Acre was once a farm among the others in southeast Westfall, near the bridge into Duskwood. When the Defias Brotherhood seized the region, they took the farm that had stood there, along with its fields.

No farmer walks it now. Mechanical Harvest Watchers stalk the ground instead, roaming where a harvest used to grow.

## The Deadmines

Beneath Moonbrook the tunnels run deep, and once they ran with gold. Before the orcs ever crossed into human lands, the Deadmines fed Stormwind's treasury -- by some accounts a third of it came from this rock. The First War emptied the mines of miners, and afterward they were left for dead, thought haunted, and untouched for a long while.

Into that silence came Edwin VanCleef, who gathered the Defias Brotherhood, former laborers turned brigands, and set them to building a juggernaught in the depths with goblin help.

In those depths VanCleef remains, guarded by his Brotherhood, while Gryan Stoutmantle gathers men at Sentinel Hill sworn to bring back his head.

## The Dust Plains

The Dust Plains lie across the southern reach of Westfall, dry ground given over to gnolls, who hold a scatter of camps there and threaten any who cross their territory.

On the eastern edge stands Mortwake's Tower, an old mage's tower whose original purpose has long since given way to newer masters.

## The Jansen Stead

The Jansen Stead sits at the northern edge of Westfall, close by the bridge into Elwynn Forest. Like the other farms of the region, it was emptied of its rightful owners when the Defias Brotherhood drove them out.

Nearby stand Farmer Furlbrow and his wife, who fled their own home at Furlbrow's Pumpkin Patch and now shelter at the stead.

## The Molsen Farm

Molsen Farm lies among the many farmsteads of Westfall held by the Defias Brotherhood. Its rows stand given over to rust and machinery, and mechanical Harvest Watchers roam the fields, striking down anyone who wanders too close.

## Westfall Lighthouse

The lighthouse stands at the far southern edge of Westfall, its beam still circling out over the water at night, though the hand that keeps it now belongs to a dead man.

A family once tended the flame, until the murloc Old Murk-Eye came out of the surf and drove them off in terror. They returned and relit the light, but Old Murk-Eye came back, and this time he brought the murlocs of the coast with him in a raid on the lighthouse. The family did not survive it. Grayson watched them die before him, unable to stop it, and it is his ghost that keeps the light now, standing sentinel outside the tower with no one left to relieve him.
