# Moonglade

Moonglade lies cradled in a ring of mountains, a hidden vale where the forest has never known corruption. At its heart rests Lake Elune'ara, named for the goddess herself, and in the town of Nighthaven upon its shore, tauren and night elves walk together without quarrel, bound by a shared reverence for nature that the Moonglade Wardens will not see broken.

It was to this vale that Malfurion Stormrage came in the days of the War of the Ancients, when the night elves stood on the edge of ruin against the Legion. Malfurion knew his people could not win alone, and he led Tyrande Whisperwind and his brother Illidan into the sacred quiet of Moonglade to beg Cenarius for his aid. The demigod answered, and after the Great Sundering reshaped the world, the keepers of the grove and the dryads who had sheltered here went out into it. Cenarius himself never left, and Moonglade became the holiest ground of druidic worship.

Beneath its countless mounds sleep the sanctums of druids past, and beneath Elune'ara's waters sleeps the demigod Omen, once beloved of Elune, his slumber uneasy.

## Lake Elune'ara

Crystal water fills the heart of Moonglade, still as glass beneath the trees. Beneath its surface lies Omen, the mad demigod, defeated long centuries past and never truly gone from these depths. The lake keeps him still, a quiet weight beneath calm water.

## Nighthaven

On the shores of Lake Elune'ara stands Nighthaven, a village of night elven stonework and moonwells, home to the druids of the Cenarion Circle. It is a quiet, sequestered place, closed to most outsiders, its people given over to druidism and the tending of the wild.

Only once a year does Nighthaven open its gates, when the Lunar Festival is celebrated and fireworks are lit over the lake in tribute to Elune's power, welcoming strangers who would otherwise never find welcome here.

But the village has seen darker nights than any festival. It was here that the Keeper Remulos faced the Nightmare dragon Eranikus, corrupted and turned against the village. Nightmare creatures fell upon Nighthaven, threatening to unmake it entirely, until Tyrande Whisperwind came at last and called down the light of Elune to cleanse the dragon of the corruption that had claimed him. The moonwells still burn with that same light.

## Shrine of Remulos

In western Moonglade stands the Shrine of Remulos, raised in honor of the Keeper who watches over that place. Within it dwells the night elf Tajarri, who guides young druids seeking to learn the aquatic form.

## Stormrage Barrow Dens

Beneath the quiet turf of Moonglade lie the barrow dens, sunk into earth that many nightelven druids never truly leave, their bodies resting while their spirits wander the Emerald Dream.

It was here that Malfurion Stormrage once slept, and the dens bear his name for it. When Tichondrius and his Dreadlords came in the Third War, they struck at Malfurion where he lay, nearly tearing his den apart. It was Tyrande Whisperwind who woke him, calling him back to the world to stand against Archimonde and the Burning Legion.

Afterward, when the Cenarion Circle spoke of raising a second World Tree, Malfurion returned to the barrow dens to meditate. Tyrande later came seeking him and found his body fallen still, as though asleep, with no waking it. The Circle judged it wiser not to move him: if his spirit still wandered the Dream searching for its way home, it would need to find the body where it had left it. So he remained there, watched over by the Cenarion Circle and the Sisterhood of Elune, his body lying still in the den that carries his name.

## Timbermaw Hold

Timbermaw Hold stands in northeast Felwood, where a tunnel bores through to Moonglade and Winterspring. It is the capital of the Timbermaw tribe of furbolgs.

When the corruption of the Burning Legion crept into the sacred forests of Kalimdor, the furbolgs fell under its influence. The Timbermaw tribe alone escaped that darkness, sheltering within their hold while they watched, helpless, as the forest they loved was twisted into a festering perversion of itself. Even after the source of that corruption was destroyed, the taint on the woods remained.

The Timbermaw are wary of all strangers now, fearing they might be mistaken for their corrupted kin and attacked on sight. Yet they know they cannot endure alone, and those who earn their trust find that the Timbermaw value friendship above all else. Their shamanistic tradition endures, rich in the crafting of armor from hide and in old knowledge of healing. Above all, the tribe longs to end the suffering of their corrupted kin, so that the ancient forest might one day ring again with the chants and laughter it has lost.
