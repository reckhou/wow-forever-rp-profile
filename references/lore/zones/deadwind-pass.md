# Deadwind Pass

Deadwind Pass cuts through the mountains of central Azeroth, joining Duskwood to the Swamp of Sorrows. A score of years ago it was counted part of the Redridge Mountains.

The Ivory Tower of Karazhan, once the dark wizard Medivh's seat of power, stands at the center of this jagged region and leaks evil magic into the land. The trees are petrified and leafless, and a terrible wind keens through the canyon. Ogres, demons, undead and a few human gypsies roam the interior -- and whispers tell that a new force has claimed the Ivory Tower, and that the ogres and demons are under its sway.

## Ariden's Camp

The camp lies abandoned north of the road through Deadwind Pass, a scatter of cold fire pits and empty bedrolls beneath the ever-dark trees. Ariden once led the Dark Riders from this ground, setting up camp here before he was cursed, and from it he tried to sell off fake artifacts he claimed had belonged to Medivh. Nothing remains now but the campsite itself.

## Deadman's Crossing

A hangman's tree stands at the crossing, a pair of corpses still swinging from its branches. Beside it a signpost bears two warnings: "Turn Back!" and "Abandon hope, all ye who enter here."

Here the road west leads toward Duskwood, the road east toward the Swamp of Sorrows, and the road south into Deadwind Pass, where Karazhan stands. The gallows and the warning were raised for anyone tempted to stray from the crossing into the pass beyond.

## Deadwind Ravine

The ravine cuts a craggy cleft through the mountains, its western road running along the upper level toward Karazhan, the tower's silhouette waiting ominous at the far end. At the ravine's end lies the southern bridge, the last crossing before the tower itself.

## Grosh'gok Compound

The Deadwind ogres claim the Grosh'gok Compound as their own, a mound of earth and stone raised in the Vice. Of the caves that pit that stretch of land, this one lies furthest south, and none of the others match it for size.

## Karazhan

Karazhan stands abandoned in southern Deadwind Pass, built upon a nexus of ley lines. The tower is best known for its last occupant, Medivh, last Guardian of Tirisfal. After Medivh was slain by Khadgar, Anduin Lothar, and Garona, the tower sealed itself off from the world. But recently Karazhan has stirred again -- an evil presence has taken it for its own, its halls crawling with spirits and demons, and the Shade of Medivh still lingers, his power so great that his magic echoes through the tower yet.

It is impossible to set eyes upon Karazhan without reflecting on the dark saga of its master. As befitting his title, the Last Guardian of Tirisfal had amassed a vast collection of writings and artifacts, dwarfing even that of the Kirin Tor. The tower is vast and dangerous, its halls thick with ghosts.

## Morgan's Plot

A small, decrepit church stands behind Karazhan, its graveyard and crypt sunk into the gloom of Deadwind Pass. The chapel has long housed things that do not rest easily, and something else still patrols the hill just below.

That a place meant for the dead should so often fail to keep them says something about what lies behind it.

## Sleeping Gorge

The Sleeping Gorge cuts through the eastern reaches of Deadwind Pass, a small chasm that travelers on the southern approach to Karazhan must pass through before the path turns east into the Vice.

## The Master's Cellar

Beneath the ruined village south of Karazhan, where servants of the tower once kept their homes, the ground opens into cellars that no longer belong to the living. Two entrances lead down. One opens onto a vast wine cellar, its racks and barrels long abandoned to the dead. The other drops into a second cellar whose floor has given way entirely, collapsing into a cave system that runs deep underground.

Both are thick with the restless: unliving caretakers who still seem to tend a house with no master, damned souls, shades that will not settle, spectres whose wailing carries up through the broken stone. Whatever service bound them to Karazhan in life has curdled into something that will not let them leave in death.

Only the wine cellar holds anything singled out among the dead. The lower cave system, silent and drowned in dark, keeps nothing but more of the same restless dead, nothing named, nothing that anyone thought worth remembering.

## The Vice

The Vice lies along the southern stretch of the road to Karazhan, where the Deadwind ogres have raised their mounds and camps among the crags. It is a desolate stretch of ground, home to little else but restless spirits and the vultures that circle over them. Why the ogres chose to root themselves here, in a place so empty and ill-favored, is not known to anyone who has looked upon it.
