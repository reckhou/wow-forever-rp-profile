# Ashenvale

Ashenvale is ancient forest, thick with old trees and old magic, and it has never stopped being fought over.

The orcs of the Warsong clan first came here as woodcutters. Goblins under Neeloc Greedyfingers stripped the trees until the local furbolgs drove them back, and Grommash Hellscream broke the furbolgs and the Sentinels in turn to keep the goblins' aid. Cenarius came himself to restore what had been felled, and it cost him his life: the Burning Legion's masters turned his trust into a trap, and Grommash struck him down.

Worse followed when the Legion marched on Ashenvale in full. Archimonde's infernals fell burning from the sky, and undead and demons swept in from the east, overwhelming the divided armies of night elf, orc, and human alike before pressing on toward Mount Hyjal. Tyrande Whisperwind woke Malfurion from the Emerald Dream to find the forest he remembered consumed by fel fire and choking blight.

That old contest never ended. Night elves still hold Astranaar and their scattered refuges; the Horde has driven its Warsong camps deep into the south and east, cutting timber and pushing the Sentinels back. Neither side has ever finished the fight for these woods.

## Astranaar

Astranaar rises from an island in the heart of Ashenvale, the largest of the region's night elven settlements, reached only by a pair of bridges that carry the road across the water. The lake sets it apart from the forest around it, a quiet, defensible place at the center of a contested land.

Its cemetery lies by the shore, holding the town's dead among the quiet rows of stone.

Beyond the town, the Thistlefur furbolgs have gathered strength in the north, and there is fear in Astranaar that they might one day find the courage to turn their strength against the town itself.

## Bathran's Haunt

The pillars of Bathran's Haunt once stood as part of a Kaldorei ruin, raised in an age when the night elves still walked this part of Ashenvale. That age passed, the elves withdrew, and the marble was left to crack and fall.

It is not empty now. Forsaken agents have taken up residence among the broken columns, herbalists and the thugs who guard them, and wherever they settle the land answers in kind: the grass has gone brown, the flowers have withered. Among the fallen stone they have raised a makeshift laboratory, working over the local herbs for purposes of their own.

Chief among what draws them here is a plant found nowhere else nearby: Bathran's Hair, a remedial herb rare enough to justify holding the site alone.

## Blackfathom Deeps

Beneath the Zoram Strand, where the ancient city of Zoram sank into the sea, lie the drowned halls of Lathar'Lazal, once a temple to Elune. The Sundering was thought to have destroyed it outright, and for millennia the ocean kept it.

Then the Twilight's Hammer came, drawn by whispers and foul dreams into the watery caverns the temple had become. The cult sacrificed untold innocents in those depths, and was rewarded with a charge: to nurture and protect a cherished creature of the Old Gods -- the monstrous hydra Aku'mai the Devourer -- until the day it is loosed upon the world. The place is called Blackfathom now, and the name fits.

## Bloodtooth Camp

Bloodtooth Camp lies west of the Falfarren River, southwest of the southern moonwell of Ashenvale. It takes its name from Ran Bloodtooth, chieftain of the furbolg tribe that holds it.

These furbolgs were once like any other of their kind, but corruption has taken every one of them, and now the camp answers with hostility to any outsider who draws near.

## Bough Shadow

Beyond Forest Song, where the trees of Ashenvale grow thick and old, Bough Shadow holds one of the portals to the Emerald Dream.

The green dragonflight keeps it. The drakes Phantim and Dreamstalker watch over the passage, and dragonspawn move through the surrounding woods in patrols of three, an Emeraldon Oracle flanked by Boughguards and Tree Warders, treading the same ground again and again in their long vigil.

## Demon Fall Canyon

Demon Fall Canyon carries the scar of the moment the orcs broke free of their old bondage.

Here Mannoroth the pit lord met Grommash Hellscream and Thrall in single struggle. The demon struck Thrall down, but could not turn aside quickly enough from Grom's axe, which drove into his chest. Mannoroth's death burst outward in fire and light, and that same fire tore the life from Grom as it claimed the demon. The orcs took the site of his sacrifice and gave it his name, for it was there the curse the Burning Legion had laid upon their people was broken.

Many orcs journey here still to honor the warrior who paid for that freedom, and a monument stands in his memory. But the ground remains fel-touched, and lesser demons now haunt the canyon.

## Demon Fall Ridge

Above Demon Fall Canyon, the ridge rises broken, its slopes and the barrow den cut into its side overrun by lesser demons. Deeper in, past the reach of daylight, waits Diathorus the Seeker, a dreadlord who rules the demonic conclave gathered in Ashenvale, holding court in the dark while lesser fiends guard the ridge above.

## Falfarren River

The Falfarren cuts Ashenvale nearly in half. To its west lie the night elves under Astranaar; to the east, the orcs hold Splintertree Post. The river runs shallow enough to wade and narrow enough to cross without much trouble, save when rains swell it past its banks, but it marks a boundary all the same, one both peoples understand without needing to speak of it.

Its waters rise on the slopes of Mount Hyjal, falling through cascades into the lake at Xavian before running on between Splintertree and Night Run, under the great bridge most travelers use to cross Ashenvale, and past Silverwing Outpost and the Moonwell of Cleansing, before slipping underground beneath the northern mountains toward the Barrens.

Near Felfire Hill the river hides another danger. Bog beasts lurk beneath its surface there, striking at anything that lingers too long in the water.

## Fallen Sky Lake

A small lake lies south of Silverwing Outpost in Ashenvale, its waters gathered around a lone island. On that island dwells a Shadethicket Oracle, an elder among the shadethicket folk who ring the shore in lesser numbers, watching over the water from the reeds and thickets around it.

## Felfire Hill

Felfire Hill still bears the scars of the Third War, when infernals fell burning from the sky and struck the earth here. Demons have lingered since, foul things denning among the stone.

The Horde has fortified both bridges leading onto the hill, holding them to keep the demons from spilling toward nearby settlements.

## Fire Scar Shrine

Fire Scar Shrine stands in southwestern Ashenvale, corrupted by the demonic influence of the Shadow Council until it became a place of great evil. The orcish warlock Ilkrud Magthrull once resided here, serving Athrikus Narassin, leader of the Cult of the Dark Strand.

Now demons infest the shrine, drawn to the taint left in the stone. Among them, a doomguard named Akkrilus is known to appear in the area from time to time, a rare and dangerous sign of how deep the corruption runs.

## Forest Song

Forest Song is an old night elf ruin in the forests of Ashenvale, long left to moss and memory. For a time it held almost no one at all.

Then the druid Kayneth Stillwind made it his own, the ruin's lone inhabitant, directing the fight against the Forsaken in the region from its stones. A forgotten place became a sentinel's post, held not by numbers but by one druid's refusal to leave it.

## Greenpaw Village

Greenpaw Village is home to the Foulweald tribe, furbolgs led by Chief Murgut and the shaman Oakpaw. The bearmen here are corrupted.

## Iris Lake

Iris Lake lies in north-central Ashenvale, east of Astranaar and west of Shael'dryn's moonwell. Once part of the unbroken night elven forest, it fell to the corruption spreading from Felwood, its water turned a sickly yellowish green. Twisted bog beasts now haunt its shores, attacking any who come seeking Elune's Tears.

## Kargathia Keep

Kargathia Keep rises among spiked barricades in the Warsong Lumber Camp of eastern Ashenvale, near the Southfury River. It serves as the seat of the Warsong Clan's officers, its position chosen so that reinforcements can be called down from Orgrimmar should the Alliance move against the camp in force.

The keep takes its name from Kargath Bladefist, the orcish hero also honored at Kargath in the Badlands and Bladefist Bay in Durotar. Overseer Gorthak commands the camp from within its walls, directing the felling of Ashenvale's timber.

## Lake Falathim

Lake Falathim lies in western Ashenvale, where the goddess's moonlight always touches the water, day or night. Night elves once came here freely to swim and fish, until murlocs overran the lake and turned it savage. The scholar Teronis was last seen near its shores and never returned.

## Maestra's Post

A tower and a single night elven hall stand quiet here, holding a handful of defenders and, strangest of all for Ashenvale, two humans among the elves: Feero Ironhand and Delgren the Purifier, the only ones of their kind at this post.

Their numbers are few, for the corruption spreading through the nearby furbolgs draws off hands that might otherwise stand watch here, leaving the post's defenders stretched thin.

## Mystral Lake

The water of Mystral Lake was once kept by spirits loyal to the night elves, bound to the lake as its wardens. Sarilus Foulborne turned them, corrupting the elementals until they answered to him instead of the elves they had once served. What waits in the lake now is not what it was.

## Night Run

Night Run sits on a naze thrust out into the land, its cliffs too sheer to climb, so that a traveler must follow the Falfarren nearly to the waters of Xavian before turning west, or else find the narrow ledge north of Raynewood. There is little to find once inside: only a scatter of broken pillars and ruined stonework, the smallest and poorest of the three satyr strongholds in Ashenvale, dwarfed by Xavian and Satyrnaar alike. The Felmusk satyr who shelter among these ruins have little need of walls when the land itself guards them. Among the rubble lies one of the soulgems of the Cult of the Dark Strand, with whom the Felmusk are allied.

## Nightsong Woods

The Falfarren River cuts the eastern woods in two, and the line it draws is the line of a war that never ends. West of its banks the Silverwing Sentinels hold ground for the Alliance; east of it, the Warsong Clan has driven its stakes into the earth, and here the fighting between the two peoples is heaviest and most constant.

Farther east, a second, nameless river marks another boundary. On one side stands Splintertree Post, the heart of Horde strength in these woods; on the other, Silverwing Grove answers for the Alliance. Beyond that river lies ground that belongs to no one for long, claimed in pieces by the night elves of Forest Song, the satyrs of Satyrnaar and Xavian, the Warsong orcs, and the Burning Legion itself. No single banner has ever flown unchallenged there.

South of the fighting, the forest has paid the price of the war. The Warsong Clan has stripped these reaches bare, felling the old trees for timber to raise its settlements, leaving open, wounded ground where ancient forest once stood unbroken.

## Raynewood Retreat

High in the boughs of the Great Tree that gives this place its name hides Ordanus, a keeper of the grove and one of the eldest sons of Cenarius. He came here to conceal himself from the Shadow Council, whose agents had marked him for death. Chief among them was Braelyn Firehand, who made his death her singular purpose and sent killers against him. Ordanus survived the attempt, and remains hidden in the tree's high branches still.

Below him, Cenarion Protectors, Blink Dragons, and the Laughing Sisters keep watch from the outpost, guarding the northern path into the blighted reaches of Felwood.

## Satyrnaar

Satyrnaar stands broken among the trees of northeastern Ashenvale, its buildings long fallen to ruin, though tents now cluster among the wreckage where the Bleakheart satyr have made their home. Of the three satyr strongholds in the forest, Satyrnaar alone remains largely intact, and its place beside the road running from Splintertree toward Azshara has made it both the easiest to reach and the most fought-over of the three.

The Bleakheart hold more than shelter here. Within Satyrnaar rests one of the soulgems of the Cult of the Dark Strand, a token of the pact binding the satyr to that cult's dark purposes.

## Silverwind Refuge

Silverwind Refuge belongs to the night elves, held under the watch of the Sentinels and the water elementals that serve alongside them. Together with Astranaar, it stands as one of the major outposts of Ashenvale, and its name is well earned: a welcoming place, its inn counted among the more comfortable to be found in the region.

## Silverwing Grove

Silverwing Grove stands in south-central Ashenvale, a stand of trees held by the Silverwing Sentinels, southwest of their outpost and west of Fallen Sky Lake. Sentinel Farsong keeps watch here among those sworn to the grove's defense.

## Silverwing Outpost

Silverwing Outpost stands on the wrong side of the Falfarren River, deep in country the orcs of Splintertree Post mean to clear. Duriel Moonfire commands it, and command here means constant war -- the orcs do not raid for plunder, but to erase the outpost and drive every sentinel from these woods for good.

## Southfury River

The Southfury rises high on Mount Hyjal, spilling from a mountain lake in a fall that drops thunderously into the lowlands. From there it runs wide and swift, cutting the land in two: it marks where Ashenvale gives way to Azshara, and further south where the Barrens ends and Durotar begins. At its mouth the river throws itself over a last cascade into the Great Sea, just northeast of the goblin port of Ratchet.

Bridges cross its length, one linking Azshara to Ashenvale, another joining Durotar to the Barrens. Just north of the westernmost of these, a single stretch of water touches all four lands at once, the only place where Ashenvale, Azshara, Durotar, and the Barrens meet.

The river still feeds the fields along its banks, still turns the soil green where it runs. But it can no longer be drunk.

## Splintertree Post

The timbers of Splintertree Post still bear the marks of the axes that raised it, for it was Grommash Hellscream and his Warsong clan who built this outpost when the orcs first pushed into Ashenvale during the Third War, felling the ancient trees for their road and their camps.

The night elves have never made peace with what the Warsong did to their forests, and the old wounds sit uneasily under the strained truce between Horde and Alliance. So Splintertree draws retaliation more than most outposts in these woods, with raids on nearby Astranaar frequent on both sides.

What remains now is a garrison, not a settlement: orcs and tauren standing scout and guard, watching the tree line for the raiders they have learned to expect.

## The Dor'Danil Barrow Den

The Dor'Danil Barrow Den has long been a resting place for night elven druids, who sleep there in body while their spirits wander free. It was the Forsaken who turned their attention to the sleepers, severing the druids from their wandering spirits. The experiment did not kill them. It drove them insane.

Now the barrow den holds no peaceful sleepers. Forsaken assassins move through it unseen, while the enraged souls of the severed druids rage through the mound with nowhere left to return to. Among them linger three spirits stronger than the rest -- Uthil Mooncall, Mavoris Cloudsbreak, and Taneel Darkwood.

## The Howling Vale

A cave opens into the vale where the Shrine of Mel'Thandris stands, its stone carved with an ancient inscription praising Elune, and above it, in Darnassian: the truth is a guiding light.

The shrine was raised by the mother of Velinde Starsong in honor of Mel'Thandris, who had been entrusted with the Scythe of Elune. When Mel'Thandris died, the scythe was laid to rest here, hidden within the shrine. It remained forgotten until Velinde, having found a book in Darnassus recounting the tale, came to the vale and uncovered it herself.

In time the shrine fell to ruin, and a tribe of worgen called the Terrowulf overran it, making the cave their den. Their howls, echoing through the stone, gave the vale the name it now carries.

## The Ruins of Ordil'Aran

Little remains of Ordil'Aran now but broken stone. The Cult of the Dark Strand once made the ruins their own, ready to fall upon anyone who came too near. That ended in blood: the Thistlefur furbolgs drove them out and claimed the ruins for themselves, and the cultists' bodies still lie scattered where they fell.

Amid the wreckage stands the Heartswood, rooted in what was once a city of some name and standing. Nothing else of it survives to say what that was.

## The Ruins of Stardust

The Ruins of Stardust lie on a lake island, sunk once in sanctity, now sunk in rot. The demonic taint seeping down from Felwood has crept into the water and stone alike, staining the island a sickly yellow-green. Bog beasts, twisted by the poisoned lake, drag themselves through the wreckage now.

Yet the stardust shrubs still grow there, untouched by the corruption around them. Their leaves are said to hold a healing virtue, the one part of the ruins that still resembles what the place must have been before the rot came.

## The Shady Nook

Shady Nook lies south of Raynewood Retreat and east of Greenpaw Village, a small valley folded quietly into Ashenvale. A cave opens into its hillside, home to a small family of bears who keep to themselves among the trees and shadow.

## The Shrine of Aessina

The Shrine of Aessina stands in southwestern Ashenvale, tucked between Maestra's Post and Astranaar.

## The Talondeep Path

The Talondeep Path is a winding tunnel joining Ashenvale to the Stonetalon Mountains, its passage uneven with small dead ends and strange hollows worn into the rock. It opens west of Mystral Lake on one end, and in the northwestern reach of Windshear Crag on the other. Nothing dwells there; it is simply a way through the mountains.

## The Zoram Strand

The waves along Zoram Strand break over what was once a city. Before the world was torn apart in the Great Sundering, Zoram stood here as a night elven town, and when the sea rose to claim its share of the shattered world, Zoram went under with it. Nothing remains above the water now but the name.

What walks the strand today are not elves but naga. They have taken the shore for their own, and the wreckage scattered along the sand, broken ships, the bodies of beached sea creatures, marks how thoroughly the coast has passed into their hands. Among the rocks and drowned hollows nearby lies Blackfathom Deeps, sunk into the earth as Zoram once sank into the sea.

The Horde holds a small foothold here at Zoram'gar Outpost, driven to it more than by choice, and has spent itself fighting the Wrathtail naga who contest every foot of the strand. Those same naga fell upon Talen, leaving the outpost with enemies pressing from more than one direction. It is a hard, wet, contested stretch of coast, claimed by an empire that drowned, and held now by creatures born of the sea that swallowed it.

## Thistlefur Village

Thistlefur Village lies in westcentral Ashenvale, north of Astranaar. It is home to the Thistlefur tribe, furbolgs turned corrupt and hostile, attacking any who wander near. Two ferocious warriors, Dal Bloodclaw and Rorgish Jowl, lead the tribe and hold the village against all who approach.

## Warsong Labor Camp

In southeastern Ashenvale, east of Fallen Sky Lake, the Warsong clan keeps a labor camp where loggers and harvesters work under orcish guard, cutting timber for the clan's holdings.

The camp stands apart from the Warsong's main strength, its guards left isolated and beyond hope of reinforcement.

## Warsong Lumber Camp

The forests of Ashenvale ring with axes at Warsong Lumber Camp, where the Warsong clan strips the ancient trees for timber. The camp is anchored by a kennel of war beasts and by Kargathia Keep, a stronghold raised to guard the cutting.

The clan's hunger for wood has not gone unanswered. Illiyana and her sisters have taken to the woods here, turning nature magic against the shredders that gnaw through the trunks, and the ground between the stacked logs has become a battlefield between Alliance and Horde.

Even so, the camp still serves its purpose: a steady train of timber moves from here to Splintertree Post, whose walls and people depend on what Warsong Lumber Camp sends them.

## Xavian

Xavian rises where a temple of the night elves once stood, its stones now given over to satyrs. Among the three satyr strongholds of Ashenvale, it is here that power gathers: Prince Raze and Geltharis both make their seat within these ruins, and it is here that the satyrs have turned to the stolen Warsong Oil for their rituals.

The place takes its name from Xavius, who long ago helped open the way for the Burning Legion through a portal drawn from the Well of Eternity in Zin-Azshari. Sargeras, displeased with how slowly that working went, twisted Xavius into the very first of the satyrs. Xavian lies near enough to Azshara that the name seems no accident.

Dryads have come against Xavian - one of them, Anilia, was found here mortally wounded after searching out a branch of Cenarius the satyrs had taken for themselves, left lying on the ground amid the ruins she could not clear.

## Zoram'gar Outpost

The Zoram'gar Outpost stands as a Horde port along the Zoram Strand of Ashenvale, its docks reaching out into the waters below.
