# Wetlands

A cold gray swamp lies below the dam, mist over its rivers and ponds, mountains hemming it in on three sides and the sea open to the west. Rubble is scattered through it, the remains of dwarven strongholds that once held this ground. Now murlocs, wild beasts, and renegade orcs hold it instead, and Menethil Harbor is the Alliance's only true foothold here.

That harbor was not always Alliance ground. When the Second War began, Orgrim Doomhammer built his invasion fleet on a small island in Baradin Bay, so his ships could strike at Lordaeron's shores without facing its ground defenses. The Wetlands paid for it: orcs and dragons fed heavily on the land, and game grew scarce. After the war the port was rebuilt and renamed Menethil Harbor, in honor of Terenas Menethil II.

Grim Batol's fall came after. The red dragonflight, enslaved to serve the Dragonmaw clan there, turned on their captors once freed, and the clan was broken, its survivors scattered into the wild. Alexstrasza judged it her flight's burden to keep the mountain sealed, and red dragonkin have stood watch over it ever since.

## Angerfang Encampment

Angerfang Encampment sprawls across central Wetlands, the largest camp the Dragonmaw clan holds in the region, home to the Angerfang orcs.

Under Nek'rosh Skullcrusher, son of Nekros Skullcrusher, the camp has become a staging ground, massing catapults meant for an assault on Menethil Harbor. The threat is real enough that Captain Stoutfist has sworn to see Nek'rosh dead and the clan's war banners torn down before the catapults roll.

## Baradin Bay

The waters of Baradin Bay wash the coast where Khaz Modan meets Lordaeron, touching the shores of Menethil Harbor and Stromgarde Keep. Murlocs and crocolisks drift in its shallows, while sharks and crabs hold the deeper water.

The bay's true history lies broken along its bottom. During the Second War, the Kul Tiran armada patrolled these waters, holding the coast against the war spreading through the Eastern Kingdoms. But Kul Tiras paid dearly for that vigilance: the nation's Third Fleet was destroyed in the fighting. Some of its wrecks still lie shattered along the coast of the Wetlands, visible in the shallows where they broke apart, while others sank entirely, settling into the dark below where none have raised them since.

## Black Channel Marsh

The road to Menethil Harbor skirts Black Channel Marsh in the southwestern Wetlands. Raptors nest throughout the fen, and the hungrier ones wander close to the roadside, so travelers passing this stretch do well to keep watch.

## Bluegill Marsh

The waters of Bluegill Marsh, in the western Wetlands, hold camps of Bluegill murlocs, who never travel alone but in twos and threes among the reeds. At the head of them stands Gobbler, a murloc fiercer than the rest, whose presence makes the marsh a place best entered with caution.

## Deepwater Tavern

The Deepwater Tavern stands in Menethil Harbor, its common room thick with the talk of sailors passing through. It is a rough place -- chairs get thrown as often as ale gets poured, brawls often started at the urging of Tapoke "Slim" Jahn.

## Direforge Hill

Direforge Hill once served as a major campsite of the Dark Iron dwarves, led by the warlock Balgaras the Foul.

## Dragonmaw Gates

The Dragonmaw Gates are guarded by the red dragonflight under the drake Axtroz, who holds the way against those who seek to reach Grim Batol.

## Dun Algaz

The tunnel through Dun Algaz is older than the quarrel that split the dwarves. When the War of the Three Hammers drove the Wildhammers from Ironforge, it was through this pass that they made their retreat.

It carried the Horde's retreat as well. Beaten back from Dun Modr, the orcs fell south across the Thandol Valley and dug in at the outpost here, and Anduin Lothar, wanting them pushed clear out of Khaz Modan, sent his forces to tear the stronghold down. The assault failed. The orcs held on, and Dun Algaz became the last ground the Horde kept in the region, a foothold that would not be pried loose.

The dwarven mountaineers who watched this pass kept mostly to the hills above it, but the Dragonmaw clan overran their post and drove them back to the upper tunnel, where they hold on still. With the lower pass lost, the road between the dwarven homelands and Menethil Harbor has grown far more dangerous.

## Dun Modr

Dun Modr stands south of Thandol Span, an old dwarven city long since fallen into ruin, its blacksmith and its brewery-inn still standing amid wreckage the Dark Iron dwarves now claim as their own.

During the Second War the orcish Horde seized the ruins and built an outpost there, using it to beat back Alliance troops and raid the farmlands round about, a vital staging ground for their armies. Stromgarde warriors struck out from the island citadel of Tol Barad to break that outpost, laying siege and driving the orcs south to their secondary hold at Dun Algaz. The Horde struck back, retook Dun Modr, and turned their strength even against Tol Barad itself. Only when the Horde broke at Capital City did Anduin Lothar drive them back through Thandol Span into Khaz Modan, and Dun Modr passed to the Alliance.

That peace did not last. With Ironforge's soldiers spent in wars fought far from home, the Dark Iron dwarves tore through Thandol Span, and Dun Modr fell soon after under the warlock Balgaras the Foul, who now holds the city his people have claimed as their own.

## Grim Batol

Grim Batol is a mountain fortress located in the eastern Wetlands. It has great walls and spires that reach toward a steel-gray sky, but the stronghold's bulk is underground. It was founded by the Wildhammer dwarves until it was cursed during the War of the Three Hammers. It was later seized by orcs to serve as a prison for the Dragon Aspect Alexstrasza until she was freed during the Battle of Grim Batol.

## Ironbeard's Tomb

Ironbeard's Tomb lies west of the road to Dun Modr, in the north-central Wetlands. A dwarven statue stands over the graveyard here, presumably carved in the likeness of Ironbeard himself.

The dead no longer rest easy in it. Oozes in black, crimson, and monstrous shades have crept into the crypt and defiled it, spreading through the graveyard until no one who wishes to bury their kin will come near the place anymore.

## Menethil Bay

Menethil Bay lies between the harbor and the cliffs of Dun Morogh, folded into Baradin Bay's inlet.

Near the bay's entrance rests the wreckage of the Flying Osprey, its broken hull settled into the water.

## Menethil Harbor

The wall is the first thing anyone notices, thick stone thrown up around a harbor town that sits half on rock, half on the sea. Four towers rise above it, watching the water and the road alike, and there is no way in but the bridge, the tide, or a hidden path running underground to the mainland.

The orcs raised the first shipyard here. After the First War, Warchief Orgrim Doomhammer chose this shore to build the fleet meant to carry the Horde past Lordaeron's ground defenses, stripping iron and timber from the dwarven mines of Khaz Modan to do it. When the Second War ended, the Alliance took the place and rebuilt it as its own, and Grand Admiral Daelin Proudmoore named it Menethil Harbor for Terenas Menethil II, King of Lordaeron.

For years afterward, raptors came out of the hills to strike at the town, never in great numbers, but often enough.

## Menethil Keep

Four towers rise above Menethil Harbor, watching the town below like stern guardians. This is Menethil Keep.

## Mosshide Fen

Mosshide Fen is a waterlogged stretch of the eastern Wetlands, its ground broken by the camps of the Mosshide gnolls. The tribe holds the fen, seizing and devouring any who wander too far into the marsh.

## Raptor Ridge

Raptor Ridge rises in the eastern Wetlands, northwest of the Dragonmaw Gates, its slopes and cavern thick with raptors. Deep within, the Razormaw Matriarch broods over her clutch.

## Saltspray Glen

Saltspray Glen lies where the Wetlands meet Baradin Bay, between Bluegill Marsh and Ironbeard's Tomb. Its name comes from the wind: when it blows hard off the bay, the ocean throws its spray far inland here, further than anywhere along this shore, until the ground itself is left salt-slicked. Raptors nest thick throughout the glen, as they do across the marsh and ridge nearby.

## Sundown Marsh

Deep in the northwest of the Wetlands lies Sundown Marsh, thick with crocolisks, gnolls, raptors, bog beasts, and oozes that keep most travelers away. At its heart stands Swiftgear Station, a small stone dwelling with a windmill, where Fradd Swiftgear and Wenna Silkbeard have made their home. The pair deal in what the swamp yields, strange items pulled from the muck, and recipes rarely found elsewhere.

## Thandol Span

Two great stone arches span the gap between the Wetlands and the Arathi Highlands, raised by dwarven hands and guarded for three generations by the Anvilmar line. They were built after the War of the Three Hammers, when Ironforge sought to keep faith with its Wildhammer kin in Aerie Peak, and the bridges became both road and symbol between the two dwarven kingdoms, carrying trade that enriched them both.

The bridges stand scarred now. Kam Deepfury and Balgaras the Foul led the Dark Iron dwarves against the Span, tearing down one of the two arches entirely and nearly taking the second. Dwarven forces fought fiercely to defend what remained and to retake the nearby town of Dun Modr, and many died in that fighting, among them Sully Balloo, Ebenezer Rustlocke, and the brother of Longbraid the Grim. The Dark Irons still hold the wreckage, and Balgaras the Foul yet lives among them.

## The Green Belt

The Green Belt is thick with crocolisks, bog beasts, and oozes, and Rethiel the Greenwarden keeps watch here, angered by a land gone strange and unquiet.

To the west, mirebeasts have turned malicious. To the northeast, fire elementals rise unlooked-for out of Direforge Hill. Both are wounds to the land Rethiel wardens, and neither has abated.

Within the Belt lies a wrecked Dragonmaw clan caravan, red whelps now denning among its ruin, all that remains of an effort to steal young of the black dragonflight that met its end here, swallowed by the marsh.

## The Lost Fleet

The wrecks of the Wave Mistress and the Intrepid lie on the seabed of Baradin Bay, off the coast of northern Bluegill Marsh.

They were part of the Kul Tiras Third Fleet, commanded by Derek Proudmoore, elder son of Lord Admiral Daelin Proudmoore, during the Second War. At the Battle of Crestfall, red dragons enslaved by the Dragonmaw clan fell out of the sky and set the fleet alight. Derek climbed to the crow's nest to warn his father of the danger. A dragon killed him there before the warning could matter, and his ship went down with the rest of the fleet soon after. Only one man survived, First Mate Fitzsimmons of the Wave Mistress.

The dead did not stay quiet. Deep in the Intrepid's hold, its commander, Captain Halyndor, had hidden away a blackened orb out of greed: the Cursed Eye of Paleth. Its curse reached into the sunken crew years later, binding them into undeath. Only First Mate Snellig kept his mind through it, and his ghost still walks the nearby shore.

## Thelgen Rock

Thelgen Rock rises in the southern Wetlands, its caverns running deep into the mountain and thick with poisonous spiders spinning silk through every crevice. Somewhere in the deeper tunnels lie veins of incendicite ore, guarded by the spiders' numbers.

## Whelgar's Excavation Site

The dig site east of Menethil Harbor was once quiet work for dwarves cutting into earth older than memory. Then mottled raptors overran it, pinning the diggers on a ridge above their own excavation. They did not hold, abandoning the site for a camp of their own making off the main road, Whelgar's Retreat.

What they left behind lies half-uncovered still. Living fossils and old elementals walk the pits now, unbothered by the picks left rusting there.
