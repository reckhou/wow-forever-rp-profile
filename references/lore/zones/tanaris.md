# Tanaris

Sand fills the place where a jungle used to stand. Before the world was broken, Tanaris was green, claimed as territory by the Gurubashi Empire. When the Great Sundering tore the land apart, the jungle died and what was left baked into dune and stone. A tribe of trolls found themselves stranded here when the water receded, and over the long years that followed, their bodies and their ways bent to the desert until they were no longer the trolls they had been, but sand trolls, the Sandfury, a people made by the land that trapped them.

The goblins of the Steamwheedle Cartel chose this wasteland for their trade, founding Gadgetzan and Steamwheedle Port because the desert is as close to their sunken homeland as Kalimdor allows them to settle. The docks at Steamwheedle reach out toward the islands beyond, but the goblins keep their ships to themselves, unwilling to ferry any other race across the water.

Beneath the dunes and the southern mountains, the tunnels run on for miles, largely unmapped, and those who go down do not always come back to say what they found.

## Abyssal Sands

The sands here lie within Tanaris, a desert reaching in every direction.

## Broken Pillar

South of Gadgetzan, near the Abyssal Sands, stands a small huddle of troll ruins known as the Broken Pillar. There the goblin scientist Marvon Rivetseeker has made his camp, searching among the fallen stones for answers of his own.

## Caverns of Time

The Caverns of Time lie beneath the eastern sands of Tanaris, a winding labyrinth sealed against the world above. Here the bronze dragonflight keeps watch over the branching paths of time itself. Since the world was young, this has been their sacred charge, laid upon them by the titans: to guard the caverns against mortal intrusion, for the meddling of lesser hands could unravel the flow of history. At the heart of the caverns stands a great hourglass.

Nozdormu, the Aspect of Time, dwells here among his flight, tending to a private hobby of collecting rare artifacts. In the aftermath of the Second War, the mage Krasus made his way into Nozdormu's lair by means of an Eon Rose, a device that let him step across the distance in an instant. Nozdormu was displeased at the intrusion, for it disturbed him in the midst of his collecting, and he made his displeasure known.

## Dunemaul Compound

Dunemaul Compound is the mound of the Dunemaul ogres, sprawled across the sands of Tanaris, where the clan gathers in its earthen shelters beneath the sun.

## Eastmoon Ruins

Stone terraces and cracked pillars mark Eastmoon Ruins, west of the Gaping Chasm in Tanaris, all that remains of a troll settlement long abandoned. Dunemaul ogres claimed the crumbling stonework after the trolls, and hold it still.

## Gadgetzan

Gadgetzan rises out of the northern Tanaris desert under a permanent blanket of black fumes, the capital of the Steamwheedle Cartel, greatest of the goblin cartels. Here the goblins' creed governs everything: profit above loyalty. Both Horde and Alliance officially recognize the city as neutral ground, and goblin bruisers patrol its streets, ready to pound to a pulp anyone foolish enough to bring war into a place built on trade. Wallets, not banners, decide who is welcome.

That neutrality has never sat easy with everyone. The Wastewander bandits took the Noonshade Ruins and the Waterspring Field to the northeast. The goblins cared nothing for the old ruins, but the Waterspring Field was another matter: the water towers raised and maintained there by the Gadgetzan Water Company, under the desert's blazing sun, were the liquid gold that kept the city alive. The goblins were not about to surrender towers they had sweated for, and so mercenaries from far corners of the world were drawn into defending Gadgetzan's water against the bandits who would see it fall.

## Land's End Beach

Land's End Beach lies at the southernmost point of Tanaris, where Kalimdor's southeastern corner meets the sea. The shore is quiet, marked mostly by the creatures that roam it. Surf gliders and duneshore crabs pick their way along the eastern stretch of sand, undisturbed.

## Lost Rigger Cove

Tucked behind mountains on three sides, Lost Rigger Cove hides the Southsea Pirates' port on the Tanaris coast, its shipyard busy with a new vessel under construction.

## Noonshade Ruins

The Noonshade Ruins lie east of Gadgetzan, worn stone left behind by trolls long since gone. What purpose the ruins once served, or what became of those who raised them, the desert sands no longer say.

## Sandsorrow Watch

Sandsorrow Watch spreads west of Gadgetzan across the dry basin of Tanaris, held by the Farraki trolls and the zombies they have raised among their own dead.

## South Seas

The waters south of the known continents open into a wide stretch of the Great Sea, carrying currents past islands whose names are known mostly to sailors. Zandalar rises from these waters, ancient home of the troll empire. Kezan lies among them too, a volcanic island holding its own people apart from the mainland powers.

Closer to Stranglethorn Vale, the sea grows treacherous with reefs and hidden coves, the sort of waters where a captain either knows every rock by name or loses the ship. Small islands dot this stretch, some no more than a sandbar, others holding ruins whose builders have long vanished beneath the waves.

What makes this sea worth naming is less any single battle fought upon it than the things it has swallowed and half-revealed again: sunken temples, drowned coasts, ruins glimpsed through clear water at low tide. It is a sea of edges and in-between places, running along the borders of Stranglethorn and Tanaris, carrying whatever the tides choose to bring to the shores it divides.

## Southbreak Shore

Southbreak Shore lies quiet along the southern edge of Tanaris, a flat stretch of beach mostly empty of life. Turtles and sea giants roam the surf.

## Southmoon Ruins

The Southmoon Ruins are the remains of an old troll settlement in southern Tanaris, their stones weathered and half-buried in the desert sand.

## Steamwheedle Port

Steamwheedle Port sits on Tanaris's eastern coast, a road running west to its mother city of Gadgetzan and a dock reaching toward Kezan. It belongs to the Steamwheedle Cartel, goblins who deal with all comers so long as the gold is good.

Among the port's residents is Yeh'kinya, whose dealings stirred the return of Hakkar the Soulflayer.

## The Gaping Chasm

The Gaping Chasm lies swarming with Hazzali silithid, its hive complexes riddled through the rock in southeastern Tanaris.

Senior Surveyor Fizzledowser once sent his junior, Scrimshank, down into the chasm to survey it. Scrimshank never came back out. He died among the hives, and only his gear was later recovered and carried back to Fizzledowser, who had sent him to his death without ever knowing it.

## The Noxious Lair

Southwestern Tanaris holds a wound in the sand where water once stood. Something broad and shallow, a river or a lake, lay across this ground before the silithid came, and what the Centipaar swarm found there it emptied. Western Tanaris was left dry for it.

The surface still shows the shape of what was lost. The land dips, sunken and hollowed, looking out over Un'Goro Crater as if it remembers being a shoreline. Here and there great claws stand erect out of the sand, twitching, as though buried creatures had frozen mid-strike. Around each claw the soil is black and wet with silithid goo, the only moisture left in a place that used to hold a lake.

Below the surface the Noxious Lair is a hive like any other the Centipaar have carved into Tanaris: humid, warm, eerie in its stillness, its tunnels coated in silithid acid.

## Thistleshrub Valley

Thistleshrub Valley lies thick with giant cacti, strange for such barren desert to sustain living growth. The thistleshrubs that once grew thick here have thinned in recent times, and desert blooms can now be found among them, though only sparsely.

## Uldum

Uldum lies on the southern coast of Kalimdor, walled in by the Un'Goro Crater to the north, Tanaris to the east, and Silithus to the northwest. It is a place rich with the lore of the titans, its mountain passes shrouded so that no mortal eye may find the way in.

## Valley of the Watchers

The Valley of the Watchers guards southern Tanaris, where a great gate stands impenetrable against Uldum. Mountain giants, the Dune Smashers, roam here as its watchers.

## Waterspring Field

Waterspring Field takes its name from the water towers that rise across it, drawing water up from reserves deep underground. The goblins of Gadgetzan built them, meaning to secure water for their desert operations in Tanaris.

That plan did not survive the Wastewanders. The nomadic bandits swept into the field and simply took it, seizing the towers for themselves without a thought for the goblins who raised them. They squat there still, glad enough of this ground after whatever they fled before -- they do not care to speak of the ogre camp that came before it, only that this is better.

## Wavestrider Beach

Wavestrider Beach runs along the eastern shore of Tanaris, the stretch of sand between Steamwheedle Port and Lost Rigger Cove. Desert Crawlers scuttle across the beach here, alongside the Steeljaw Snappers that share the same shoreline.

## Zalashji's Den

Zalashji's Den still bears the name of whoever once denned here. Nothing in the tales that survive says who Zalashji was, or what became of the place after. Whether Zalashji still lives, or died here, or elsewhere, no one can say for certain.

## Zul'Farrak

Zul'Farrak was once a city of jungle, when Tanaris was still forest under the Gurubashi Empire. When the Great Sundering tore the ancient world apart, the jungle burned away into desert, and the Farraki trolls who had built their city here found themselves stranded amid the sand. They endured, hardening into the Sandfury, a tribe known even among trolls for its ruthlessness and its dark mysticism.

Within the sun-blasted walls, troll legend speaks of Sul'thraze the Lasher, a blade said to instill fear and weakness in any foe it touched. Long ago the sword was broken in two, and it is whispered that both halves still lie somewhere inside the city, lost among its courts and temples.

At the heart of Zul'Farrak lies a sacred pool, and beneath its still water the Sandfury are said to keep something far older than themselves sleeping - a demigod whose waking would bring ruin down upon the city and all within it.
