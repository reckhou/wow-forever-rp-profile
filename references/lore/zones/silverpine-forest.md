# Silverpine Forest

Silverpine Forest, or simply Silverpine, is a vast, ancient wood that runs along Lordaeron's rugged western coast. The land ranges from fairly flat to hilly, and is nestled among even taller mountains. The forest is eerily silent save for the occasional unnatural howling echoing among the trees. Mossy overhangs shiver in the cold breeze, and the trees are all sickly or dying. Dilapidated farmsteads and abandoned mines dot the land, home now to the darker denizens of the woods.

This woodland is haunted and wild, characterized by its tall, silver-barked pines towering over grassy knolls. The Alliance once protected Silverpine Forest when it was verdant and lively. Now the place is shrouded in ghostly mists. Forsaken control the northern forest and constantly clash with the wretched Rot Hide gnolls. What remains of the human population retains control of the southern forest, but they are desperate and their forces thinly stretched. Dalaran sends frequent expeditions into Silverpine to defend these people and study the undead plague and worgen curse -- a mysterious affliction that causes its victims to transform into bloodthirsty, lycanthropic worgen when the moon rises.

Gilneas and the Hillsbrad Foothills border the forest to the south, while Tirisfal Glades lays to the north. Lordamere Lake serves as Silverpine's eastern border.

Though the territory belongs to the Forsaken, their claim is disputed by the wizards of Dalaran and agents of the Scourge who have taken possession of some of the ruined fields and steadings. Rothide gnolls operate from the mysterious Fenris Isle, and fiercely attack anyone who dares approach their dealings. In the southwest lays Pyrewood Village, whose people have fallen under archmage Arugal's curse, transforming into bloodthirsty worgen at night-time. Arugal makes his home in the nearby darkened towers of Shadowfang Keep.

Silverpine Forest frequently sees battle, as Alliance forces from Southshore journey to the forsaken woods to attack the Sepulcher or enter Shadowfang. It is the edge of the Forsaken homeland, and the southernmost point where effects of the undead plague can be seen in the plant life.

## Ambermill

Ambermill was once a mill town of no importance, cutting timber southwest of Lordamere Lake in Silverpine Forest.

The Scourge and the Burning Legion changed what the town was worth. The Kirin Tor of Dalaran, rebuilding after their own ruin, occupied the town, and for a time Ambermill became something it had never been: a source of real human resistance in Silverpine Forest, propped up by Dalaran's wizardry.

The Forsaken have not let that stand unwatched. Their Deathstalkers have learned, through a prisoner, that the wizards keep documents there bearing on the state of Dalaran itself. The mills, the stables, and the town hall remain in Kirin Tor hands, while spies move against them from the shadows of the forest.

## Beren's Peril

In the hills southwest of Ambermill lies Beren's Peril. Years after the Third War, Forsaken scouts brought word to Shadow Priest Allister that undead had gathered there in secret, massing for a strike against the Sepulcher. The undead served the necromancer Thule Ravenclaw, who had chosen the place to gather his servants unseen, close enough to threaten the Sepulcher, far enough from the road to escape notice.

## Deep Elem Mine

Deep Elem Mine lies in Silverpine Forest. Arugal let his magic spread into it, and worgen made the mine their den, led by Grimson the Pale, once foreman there.

Dalar Dawnweaver sought Grimson's death, so that the Forsaken might claim the mine as their own resource, in Varimathras's name.

## Fenris Isle

Fenris Isle sits in the middle of Lordamere Lake, one piece of a broken ring of dry land it shares with the Dawning Isles, and it is counted, loosely, as part of Silverpine Forest.

Before the Third War, the island held Fenris Keep, a military outpost of Lordaeron. When the Scourge swept over the land, the keep fell. Its people either fled or were killed where they stood, and the isle was left empty.

Into that emptiness came the Rot Hide gnolls, who took the abandoned keep as their den. Their master was Thule Ravenclaw, remembered as treacherous even among gnolls, who rules the Rot Hides and, through them, all of Fenris Isle, holding the keep he claimed and the nearby Decrepit Ferry besides.

## Fenris Keep

Fenris Keep stands at the center of Lordamere Lake, upon Fenris Isle in Silverpine Forest, once home to a sizable portion of Lordaeron's military.

When the Scourge swept over the land, the keep fell with it. Into the ruin came Thule Ravenclaw, a young mage once of the Kirin Tor, who threw in with the Scourge and bent the isle's gnolls to his will, forging them into the Rot Hide. With that tribe at his back, he made himself master of Fenris Keep, and the Rot Hide fall on anyone who nears their master.

## Lordamere Lake

Lordamere Lake spreads beneath the shadow of kingdoms that once called its shores home. Lordaeron rose on its northern edge, and it is very likely from that great city, itself named for old Lordain, that the lake took its name. Dalaran and Alterac, too, stood prosperous around its waters in the days when the Alliance of Lordaeron bound the human kingdoms together.

That age is gone, and what remains is a border. The lake now lies between the Forsaken holdings of Tirisfal Glades and Silverpine Forest and the contested Alterac Mountains, separating the dead from the living.

At its heart sits Fenris Isle, its keep held by the Rot Hide gnolls under Thule Ravenclaw.

East of Fenris Isle, among the small Dawning Isles, the Vile Fin murlocs have made their claim, overrunning them entirely.

## Malden's Orchard

The trees in Malden's Orchard have gone dark and evergreen, their fruit hanging unpicked season after season. Malden himself is long gone, driven from his home when the Scourge came down over Lordaeron.

Now the orchard belongs to the worgs, denning in numbers among the trees and around the single small cottage that still stands there. It was into this that the Deathstalker Erland McKree walked, sent to scout the orchard during a reconnaissance of the northern forest. The worgs drove him to ground before he had gone far, and he barricaded himself inside the abandoned cottage, pinned there for days, until other Forsaken agents helped him escape back to his waiting partners.

## North Tide's Hollow

North Tide's Hollow lies just north of the Sepulcher in Silverpine Forest. It is one of the many haunts of the Moonrage worgen, servants of the Archmage Arugal. Here they raise crude encampments, holding nothing but the meanest necessities needed for survival, shelter enough for creatures who live by tooth and strength.

## North Tide's Run

The northwestern shoreline of Silverpine Forest, where the beach meets the cold Great Sea, bears the name North Tide's Run. Half-sunk in the shallows lies a broken Horde ship, its hull settled into the surf, the most prominent mark upon this stretch of coast.

## Olsen's Farthing

Olsen's Farthing once stood at the edge of the trade roads running through Silverpine Forest, a working farmstead whose fields fed the traffic passing by.

The Third War ended that. Within a few years the farm lay in ruins.

## Pyrewood Village

Pyrewood Village stands on the edge of Silverpine Forest, a huddle of humans who lived through the Scourge's first coming during the Third War. To the north stands a keep once held by Baron Silverlaine, guarded by the paladin Commander Springvale and the Silver Hand. Silverlaine and his men were slaughtered by the worgen of Archmage Arugal, who took the keep for his own and renamed it Shadowfang Keep. From there Arugal turned his madness on Pyrewood itself, cursing its people so that every night they become slavering Moonrage worgen, tearing at anyone who crosses their path, only to wake human again come dawn.

Despite this, the village still opens its doors to travelers by day, its people bound to the curse Arugal laid upon them, with no end to it in sight.

## Shadowfang Keep

Shadowfang Keep stands on a high bluff above Pyrewood Village. It belonged once to Baron Silverlaine, a lord of Gilneas, until the Scourge came down upon Lordaeron and the Royal Archmage Arugal sought a way to stop them. Drawing on the research of the mage Ur, and with King Genn Greymane's blessing but against the counsel of the Kirin Tor, Arugal summoned the worgen. They turned back the undead tide, then turned on Gilneas itself, savaging the Greymane Wall before running loose through Silverpine. They fell upon the keep and killed Baron Silverlaine and his household.

Arugal, broken by what he had unleashed, did not flee it. He named the worgen his children, took the keep for his own, and renamed it Shadowfang. Communing with a master he served in the Wolf Cult, he spread the curse through the forest, until Pyrewood Village itself was turned, its people human by day and beasts by night, ruled over by a council that answered to him alone.

## South Tide's Run

South Tide's Run runs along the southwestern coast of Silverpine Forest, where the land meets the Great Sea.

## The Dawning Isles

The Dawning Isles once rose gentle and low from the crystalline waters of Lordamere Lake, quiet keys untouched by trouble. The Plague of Undeath changed that. Their trees sickened, and murlocs and bog beasts overran the islets. The bog beasts are gone now, but the Vile Fin murlocs bred unchecked and now hold the isles entirely.

## The Dead Field

The Dead Field lies abandoned in northern Silverpine Forest, its fields gone to rot and its original tenants forgotten. What is known is what has claimed it since: the Rot Hide gnolls, bound in service to the Scourge, who have made the ruined farm their base, their numbers swelling in a buildup that suggests some assault still being readied here.

The Forsaken have struck at the farm more than once, hoping to break the gnolls before they can move. Every attempt has failed. Whenever the Rot Hides come under threat, a banshee answers their call, a spirit named Nightlash who commands the gnolls as her own, appearing out of nothing to defend them.

## The Decrepit Ferry

The Decrepit Ferry once carried trade out to Fenris Isle, where a lord and his family kept their manor, paying the ferryman a few coins a trip.

Then the Scourge came down over Lordaeron. The ferryman was slain where he worked, and his ferry broken apart.

Now ghouls, skeletal warriors, and Rot Hide gnolls haunt the ruined docks, and the crossing stands abandoned, dangerous to any but the strong.

## The Greymane Wall

King Genn Greymane raised his wall before the Third War, weary of Gilnean coin spent on the orc internment camps and worn down by the quarrels dividing the kings of the Alliance of Lordaeron. He turned his kingdom inward, cutting Gilneas off from Lordaeron and from the world beyond the mountains.

The wall held against the Plague of Undeath, but at a cost. As the Scourge swept over Lordaeron, refugees fled south and camped outside the gates, and for years the gates did not open. The refugees perished there, unanswered.

## The Ivar Patch

Ivar's Patch was once a farm worth having, its rows heavy with pumpkins, its house full of hired hands who worked the fields for a man named Ivar. It was a good living, until the Plague of Undeath came down over Lordaeron and took it from him.

Ivar did not simply die. He rose, and so did his family, and so did every farmhand who had worked his land, all turned to rotting ghouls that now fall on anyone who comes near. Ivar himself rose as something worse, remembered now as Ivar the Foul.

Three deathstalkers sent north to judge the danger were caught here by the ghouls, and Ivar the Foul wounded one of them, Quinn Yorick, before Quinn and his kinswoman Rane barricaded themselves inside Ivar's old house.

## The Sepulcher

The Sepulcher sits above the Lordaeronian coastline in the western hills of Silverpine Forest, where a road once crossed a simple graveyard, its crypts marking nothing more than a resting place for the dead.

After the Third War, the Forsaken took the old graveyard and converted it into a base of operations, turning a place built for the dead into one that served the undying.

The choice came from necessity. Worgen prowled to the south, the Scourge still held ground to the east, and the Kirin Tor watched from the southeast. Surrounded on nearly every side, the Forsaken found in the Sepulcher one of the few places in Silverpine where the Horde could stand in safety.

## The Shining Strand

The Shining Strand runs along Lordamere Lake, from the capital of Lordaeron in the north down to the rough hills dividing Silverpine Forest from the Alterac Mountains.

The Vile Fin tribe of murlocs holds the strand thick with their settlements, and travelers who do not know the shoreline have learned to fear it. The late Apothecary Berard, in his journal, wrote of a rare item to be had from the Vile Fins, a Hardened Tumor, and speculated that it might be worked into a new plague.

## The Skittering Dark

The mine known as the Skittering Dark has been claimed by Skitterweb spiders, their webs draped over broken timbers and rusted tools. Deep within hides the Skitterweb Matriarch, a spider grown monstrous, ruling the swarm from a dark she never leaves. Outside, the rare Krethis the Shadowspinner sometimes stalks the rocks alone.

Cocoons litter the ground, binding worgen, worgs, and bears, along with orcs dragged in from a wreck along the shore to the west.

## Valgan's Field

Valgan's Field lies among the desolate farms of northern Silverpine Forest, west of the Shining Strand and north of a small river. It takes its name from a farmer who was killed or driven from the land when the Scourge swept through Silverpine. In its better days the farm looked out over Lordamere Lake, a view worth the name it once had.

The farm stands empty now, a ruin of what it once was, though Nightlash can occasionally be spotted about the place.
