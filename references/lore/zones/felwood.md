# Felwood

The trees of Felwood do not grow so much as fester. Their bark weeps a sickly ichor, their leaves hang black and curling, and the wildlife beneath them comes twisted and hungry from the corruption in the soil.

The rot was no accident of nature. As the Burning Legion pressed toward Mount Hyjal, Archimonde ordered the forest broken to weaken the night elves guarding the mountain, and the dreadlord Tichondrius carried out that order with a warlock relic of terrible power, the Skull of Gul'dan. The land answered by dying from the inside out: ancients turned rotten and violent, beasts mutated, the earth itself poisoned.

It was here that Illidan Stormrage made the choice that shaped the rest of his life. Told that the skull could be turned against the very corruption it had caused, Illidan went into Felwood alone, cut down Tichondrius, and claimed the relic for himself. He did not surrender it afterward. His hunger for magic proved stronger than caution, and for using the Legion's own power he was cast out by his own brother Malfurion.

## Bloodvenom Falls

Bloodvenom Falls tumbles down from the high places of Felwood, its waters fouled at the source. From here the Bloodvenom River is born, carrying the taint downward until it spills into Darkshore as the sickened Wildbend River.

## Bloodvenom Post

Bloodvenom Post stands on the high cliffs above Darkshore, where the Bloodvenom River cuts through Felwood. Its architecture and the tauren who guard it mark it as a tauren founding, a small Horde toehold watching over the coast below.

It is reached by a dark trail that follows the river's south bank westward from the stone path running through Felwood, curling around the hillside to the post itself.

## Bloodvenom River

The Bloodvenom River runs black through Felwood, its corruption spreading beyond the zone until it surfaces again as the Wildbend River in Darkshore. Noxious oozes crawl its banks, and the wildlife nearby has turned venomous. No one drinks from it, and no one swims in it.

## Deadwood Village

The Deadwood furbolgs built their village in southern Felwood behind walls of heavy timber, sturdier structures raised within. Like their kin across the corrupted forest, they succumbed to Felwood's taint, turning hostile and twisted.

## Emerald Sanctuary

The Emerald Sanctuary stands in southeastern Felwood, a camp of the Emerald Circle druids. From here they watch Jaedenar, working to snuff out the demonic corruption spreading from it before it consumes more of the land.

## Felpaw Village

Felpaw Village stands at the northern edge of Felwood, near the entrance to Timbermaw Hold. It belongs to the Deadwood, furbolgs whose corruption has turned them from their kin, and their taint still marks the ground they hold.

## Irontree Cavern

Deep within the cave, the twisted wood of the Warpwoods has taken root, giving rise to Shredders and Moss Flayers that stalk the dark in place of any living guardian.

## Irontree Woods

The Irontree Woods once stood as a peaceful glade in northern Felwood, before the corruption of the Burning Legion twisted it into something else. Three great Ancients of the night elves lived here, living trees named Hastat, Stoma and Vartrus. The corruption did not spare them: all three were petrified where they stood and remain entombed in the woods still, dead among the blight that killed them.

Yet something has refused to stay dead. Moonwell water poured into the heart of the woods gave rise to a seedling. Tended and grown, it blossomed, then returned to the ground where it was born, rooted among the petrified dead, waiting to stand as an Ancient in their place.

## Jadefire Glen

In the corrupted woods of southern Felwood, Jadefire Glen shelters a camp of satyr, demonic creatures who have made the tainted hollow their own, thriving amid its rot and spreading the corruption further through the trees around them.

## Jadefire Run

In northern Felwood lies Jadefire Run, home to the most powerful satyrs in the region. Caution is warranted for any who venture near. Its structures follow demonic rather than mortal design.

## Jaedenar

Jaedenar rises out of ground that once belonged to the night elves, its stones older than the corruption that now claims them. Beneath its ruins lie two druidic barrow dens, sacred once, and it is on this desecrated foundation that the Shadow Council built its dominion, naming the place itself in worship of Kil'jaeden the Deceiver.

After the Third War, the ruins passed into the hands of Lord Banehollow and the Shadow Council he led. Orcish and Forsaken warlocks filled its halls, and they did not come alone: succubi, felhunters, felguards, and satyrs moved among them. At its heart stands the Shadow Hold, the Council's seat of power on Azeroth, and beside it the Shrine of the Deceiver, raised in Kil'jaeden's name.

## Morlos'Aran

Morlos'Aran once stood as a great night elf settlement, raised before the Great Sundering tore the world apart. Now it lies in ruin at the southern edge of Felwood, one among several such places, like Ameth'Aran, Bashal'Aran, Ordil'Aran, and Tethris'Aran, all once thriving cities, all now only stone and memory of a golden age long lost.

## Ruins of Constellas

The broken columns here once belonged to a night elven settlement near Mount Hyjal. A moonwell still rests at its heart, but it no longer holds clean light. Fel corruption has soaked into its waters, and the satyr Xavathras now tends it on behalf of Xavaric, cultivating the corruption like a garden meant to poison. Deeper in the ruins, a wall of fire bars the way to him.

Druidic kin have not left the demons unchallenged, striking at them in an effort to tear the place back from the fel that has taken root in it.

## Shadow Hold

Beneath Jaedenar, in western Felwood, lies a labyrinth that was once a night elf barrow den. The Shadow Council claimed it long ago, and now warlocks and their demonic familiars roam its tunnels. The satyr Prince Xavalis and the dreadlord Lord Banehollow both dwell within.

The paladin Trey Lightforge and the night elf Arko'narin Starshade ventured into Felwood hunting signs of the Shadow Council's work. The cultists took them both and caged them within Shadow Hold. Trey was tortured to death there, his spirit twisted and corrupted by his captors. Arko'narin remains caged in the depths, watched by her tormentors, waiting for a rescue that has not yet come.

## Shatter Scar Vale

Shatter Scar Vale is where the infernals fell when the Burning Legion tore through Felwood during the Third War. The craters they left still smolder, scarring the earth, and demons yet wander among them.

## Shrine of the Deceiver

The Shrine of the Deceiver, a corrupted barrow den at Jaedenar in Felwood, takes its name from Kil'jaeden, the Deceiver, lord of the Burning Legion. Warlocks of the Shadow Council fill its depths, making it a dangerous place for the unprepared.

## Talonbranch Glade

Talonbranch Glade stands in the northern reaches of Felwood, south of Timbermaw Hold, along the path that leads on to Moonglade and Winterspring.

## Timbermaw Hold

Timbermaw Hold, in northeast Felwood, is the capital of the Timbermaw tribe of furbolgs. Its tunnels open a passage through to Moonglade and Winterspring.

When the corruption of the Burning Legion crept into the sacred forests of Kalimdor, the furbolgs fell under its influence and were twisted into madness. The Timbermaw tribe alone escaped that darkness, sheltering within their hold while the forest they loved was turned into a festering perversion of itself. Even long after the demons' leader was struck down, the taint on the woods remained.

The furbolgs of Timbermaw Hold are wary of all strangers, fearing they may be mistaken for their corrupted kin and attacked on sight. Yet they know they cannot endure alone, and those they come to trust find that the Timbermaw value friendship above all else. Above all, they wish only to end the suffering of their corrupted brethren and find some cure for the wound Felwood has become.
