# Durotar

Durotar is a cracked, red land of crags and canyons, dry and hot, sparse in water and green things. Orcs find in it a harsh beauty that recalls Draenor, and it is here, after Mount Hyjal, that Thrall led his people to build a new homeland, naming it for his murdered father Durotan. The land had belonged to the Razormane quilboar before the Horde drove them off.

That peace did not hold long. Grand Admiral Daelin Proudmoore, a hero of the Second War and no friend to orcs, brought a great Alliance fleet against Durotar, determined to crush the Horde before it could take root. His daughter Jaina chose otherwise: at Theramore she turned against her own father to fight beside Thrall, and Daelin died in the battle before the two could ever reconcile. In gratitude for her loyalty, Thrall let Jaina's soldiers withdraw to Theramore unharmed.

The Darkspear trolls came to Durotar's coast as exiles, not settlers by choice. Betrayed by one of their own, the sorcerer Zalazane, they were driven from their home in the Echo Isles and built the fishing village of Sen'jin on the mainland shore, never able to reclaim what Zalazane had taken.

## Bladefist Bay

The waters east of Durotar carry Kargath Bladefist's name, a namesake of the Shattered Hand clan's former chieftain. The coast of Azshara can be seen to the north across the water, and Skull Rock rises along this stretch of shore.

The bay has earned a rougher name among those who know it: the Great Sea's midden heap. The tide brings in the sea's rubble again and again, wrack and wreckage washing up near Skull Rock, so the shore never stays clean for long.

## Burning Blade Coven

The mountains walling the northern edge of the Valley of Trials once held nothing worse than stone and shadow. Now a cave mouth spills imps and lesser demons into the valley, and stronger things are said to linger deeper in the abandoned caverns beyond, among them members of the Burning Blade itself. A warlock named Yarrog Baneshadow is said to command what dwells within.

Before the demons came, a foreman named Thazz'ril walked into those caverns to survey them for minerals. He left his pick behind when he was done, and it remains there still.

For the orcs and trolls who prove themselves in the Valley of Trials, the Coven has become the final test before leaving the valley behind.

## Darkspear Strand

The Darkspear trolls came ashore here when they fled the Echo Isles. From this strand they made their way inland to Sen'jin Village, where the survivors gathered to begin again.

## Deadeye Shore

Deadeye Shore takes its name from Kilrogg Deadeye, the orc hero who once led the Bleeding Hollow clan. A great patch of sand torn from the desert here is shaped like a skull missing an eye, as if the land bore the wound his name recalls.

Little disturbs the shore now but raptors, scorpids, and boars. The orc fisherman Byrok once worked these waters, until the Burning Blade fell upon him here.

## Drygulch Ravine

Drygulch Ravine lies sunken below the land around it, reachable only by falling down its walls or through a narrow cave from Razorwind Canyon. A river runs through it, feeding the dry, cracked ground that gives the place its name.

The ravine now belongs to the Dustwind harpies. They have turned to banditry, falling on caravans and dragging the plunder up to nests on the high cliffs, where the strongest among them stand guard over their eggs.

## Dustwind Cave

Atop the cliffs of Razorwind Canyon, northeast of Razor Hill, a narrow cave opens into the rock. It is small, easily overlooked, but the Burning Blade uses it well: here, cultists drill those newly sworn to the cult, shaping them before they are trusted elsewhere in Durotar.

## Echo Isles

The Echo Isles rise off the coast of Durotar, tropical land thick with raptors and lynxes, home to the Darkspear tribe of jungle trolls. Once, at the height of the Gurubashi Empire, jungle trolls held these waters as part of a realm stretching down to Stranglethorn Vale.

After Warchief Thrall saved the Darkspears and brought them into the Horde, the tribe settled the isles anew. Their peace did not last. Zalazane, a witch doctor of their own tribe, had learned the secrets of undeath, and turned zombies and hexed kin against the Darkspears who trusted him. He buried tainted fetishes among his huts on the main isle, twisting the flow of magic through Durotar and corrupting the wildlife and dark places of the land.

Again and again the Darkspears have tried to end him, and again and again he has deceived them into believing him slain, while his undead trolls creep to the mainland to drag more of the tribe under his sway. The Darkspears remain in exile from the land Thrall once bestowed upon them, watching Zalazane's power fester upon the isles that were meant to be their home.

## Hidden Path

A hidden track winds through the mountains walling the Valley of Trials, known only to those who seek it. Shaman climb it to reach the Spirit Rock, a place of power unreachable by any easier road, and from its height the whole of the Barrens spreads below. Lower down, the path opens toward Ratchet and Orgrimmar, a quiet way in for those who know it, and for outsiders too.

## Jaggedswine Farm

Jaggedswine Farm lies near the gates of Orgrimmar, its storage house, wide pig pen, and pond feeding the swine that sustain the Horde in Durotar.

## Kolkar Crag

Kolkar Crag is a craggy gorge in southern Durotar, named for the Kolkar centaurs who hold it.

From here Warlord Kolkanis gathered his kind for a single, ambitious plan: to strike at the heart of orcish Durotar, from the Valley of Trials and Sen'jin Village to Razor Hill and even the gates of Orgrimmar itself.

## Razor Hill

Razor Hill stands where the roads of Durotar meet. South, the way runs toward Sen'jin Village and the Valley of Trials; north lies Orgrimmar; west the road crosses the river toward the Barrens and on to the Crossroads. The Razor Hill Barracks rises as the town's most prominent structure, and the settlement keeps a burrow besides.

It was here, shortly after the Third War, that the forces of Kul Tiras proposed a summit with Thrall, offering peace. Thrall suspected otherwise, and rightly so: the summit was an ambush laid for him. Rexxar, hearing this, refused to let Thrall walk into it and went to the meeting in his place. The assassins waiting at Razor Hill found Rexxar instead, and he cut them down.

Orgnil Soulscar keeps watch here still, tracking the movements of the Burning Blade in the lands around the hill.

## Razor Hill Barracks

The barracks stands as the largest building in Razor Hill, its size alone marking the settlement as one of the true strongholds of Durotar, second only to Orgrimmar itself.

## Razormane Grounds

Bones of stripped animals litter the Razormane Grounds, and brambles burst from the red mountains above. Here the Razormane quilboar push out of the Barrens into Durotar, and the orcs fight to hold their homeland's edge. For every quilboar cut down, two more claw out of the thickets to take its place. The tribe's encampment answers to Geolord Mottle, while Captain Flat Tusk commands the Battleguard that holds the line against the orcs.

## Razorwind Canyon

The canyon splits into two hollows flanking the road between Razor Hill and Orgrimmar, its walls riddled with paths and hidden turns. It is claimed by the Dustwind harpies, who have made it their nesting ground, and their numbers fill the rocks on both sides of the road. They do not tolerate trespassers lightly; the bird women strike hard and fast at anything that wanders too far from the road, and more than one traveler has been dragged down before realizing how many of them were watching. A cave in the eastern reach of the canyon opens further into Drygulch Ravine, a passage few care to take given what nests along the way.

## Rocktusk Farm

Rocktusk Farm sits just down the western road outside Orgrimmar's gates. The owners work without much fear here, raising pigs whose meat feeds the Horde.

## Scuttle Coast

The Scuttle Coast lies where Durotar meets the Great Sea, its rocks still hung with the wreckage of Kul Tiras. Here Admiral Daelin Proudmoore's fleet broke against the orcs, and the ships that did not sink were driven ashore and smashed apart.

Gnomish stores still ride in those broken holds, and the orcs comb the timbers for what can be salvaged. But sea elementals ride in on the tide and turn on anything that comes near the shore, and the orcs must fight the water as much as the wreckage to hold the coast.

## Sen'jin Village

Sen'jin Village sits on the southern coast of Durotar, a small cluster of huts raised by the Darkspear tribe. It is a modest place: three very small huts, two small huts, one larger hall, and a dock reaching into the sea where the trolls draw their living from the water.

The village keeps to old ways. Its people are healers and gatherers of herbs, alchemists who know the plants of the coast and the troll medicine passed down through generations, and it is to them that the tribe's knowledge of roots and remedies is entrusted.

## Shrine of the Dormant Flame

The Shrine of the Dormant Flame sits atop the tallest mountain of the region, reached by a secret path spiraling up from near the Southfury River, northwest of the Valley of Trials. It is holy ground to the shaman of the Horde. The climb is not one taken lightly, nor is the drop from its peak, which looks out over the whole region.

At the top waits a dormant brazier. Aspiring shaman come here to prove mastery over fire, seeking out and defeating a fire elemental, then carrying back a single ember to light the brazier as proof the spirit has submitted to their will.

Telf Joolam guides those seeking the trial.

## Skull Rock

Skull Rock, in northeastern Durotar, is a holdout of the Burning Blade cult. Within its depths, the orc warlock Gazz'uz leads the cult's remnants.

## Southfury River

The Southfury rises high on Mount Hyjal, spilling from a mountain lake in a fall that drops thunderously into the lowlands. From there it runs wide and swift, cutting the land in two: it marks where Ashenvale gives way to Azshara, and further south where the Barrens ends and Durotar begins. At its mouth the river throws itself over a last cascade into the Great Sea, just northeast of the goblin port of Ratchet.

Bridges cross its length, one linking Azshara to Ashenvale, one joining Durotar to the Barrens. Somewhere along the river's course, a single stretch of water touches all four lands at once, the only place where Ashenvale, Azshara, Durotar, and the Barrens meet.

## Spirit Rock

High above the Valley of Trials, where the Hidden Path climbs into the hills of Durotar, Spirit Rock stands sacred to the shaman of the Horde. There, aspiring shaman drink a sapta beside the stone, seeking to commune with the earth.

## The Den

In the Valley of Trials, young orcs make their first camp in a cave known as the Den. Within it dwell elders who teach the young their callings, guiding the first steps of every orc who takes up arms.

## Thunder Ridge

Thunder Ridge is a gorge in northwestern Durotar, southwest of Orgrimmar. It holds a forest of ancient trees that grew in harmony with the thunder lizards denning among them.

The harmony broke when a human lumber operation began stripping the ridge of its trees, driving the lizards mad. Rexxar, sent to gather shimmerweed for Drek'Thar, noticed the change in the beasts and carried word back to Orgrimmar. He and Drek'Thar returned together, found the logging operation, and were forced to put down the maddened lizards to avert disaster, though Rexxar gathered their eggs afterward for Drek'Thar to study.

Jaina Proudmoore, sent by Thrall to learn what had further troubled the ridge, found that its trees had been cut down by demonic magic, the work of the Burning Blade seeking to destabilize the region. A small band of its cultists, led by Fizzle Darkclaw, made camp there to spread further chaos, and Orgnil Soulscar called for Fizzle's death and his claw as proof of it.

## Tiragarde Keep

Tiragarde Keep rises white against the red rock of the Scuttle Coast, a stronghold that should never have stood in Durotar at all.

It was raised in haste, on the orders of Grand Admiral Daelin Proudmoore of Kul Tiras. The Third War had ended in an uneasy peace between Thrall and Lady Jaina Proudmoore, the Admiral's own daughter, but Daelin trusted no orc and honored no truce signed in his name. He brought his fleet from Kul Tiras to the shores of Durotar and threw it against the Horde, building Tiragarde Keep as a foothold for the war he meant to finish. The offensive broke against the orcs, and the keep fell into ruin with it.

Daelin himself did not live to see it rebuilt; he was slain when the Horde, led by Rexxar, stormed Theramore. But before sailing from Kul Tiras, he had left behind sealed orders against the day his fleet fell silent. When no word came back, those orders surfaced, and Lieutenant Benedict was sent to reclaim the ruin and carry on his commander's crusade.

So the keep stands again, battered but held, a gleaming wrong-colored scar on the crimson stone.

## Tor'kren Farm

Tor'kren Farm lies between Thunder Ridge and the Southfury Watershed, a modest holding that has never yielded the profit of its neighbors. It was once worked by Misha Tor'kren and her son Kron, until Kron went down to the Southfury River to hunt crocolisks and did not come home. Misha keeps the farm alone now, and asks for a necklace strung from crocolisk teeth, to remember him by.

## Valley of Trials

The Valley of Trials lies walled in southern Durotar, a bowl of scorched earth where every young orc begins the life the Horde has set before them. Those who fail are not carried out. They are left where they fall, to bake in the sun until the desert has finished what the trial started.

At the heart of the valley stands the Den, a cave cut into the cliffside and fitted out with the plain comforts of a life lived in service. Elders keep it, teaching the young their callings and setting them to their trials.

To the north, a modest run of natural caves once sat empty in the cliffs. Something has since crept into them. Demons of the Burning Blade now nest there, spilling out into the valley proper, and their presence has given weight to old whispers that the Shadow Council has found some purchase in the region. The demons are weak, but they do not stay in their caves, and the taint they carry has begun to spread beyond the stone that once held it.
