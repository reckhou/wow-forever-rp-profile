# Tirisfal Glades

The sky over Tirisfal Glades has hung sickly green for years, the trees clinging to whatever life the plague left them. Once these were peaceful farms under King Terenas, generations living quietly among the mills and pumpkin fields. That ended when Arthas Menethil ransacked Lordaeron and swept the plague through the countryside, killing its people and raising them as slaves to the Lich King. Mass graves still mark where the dead were too numerous to bury properly. It was near the Balnir Farmstead, months later, that Sylvanas Windrunner turned her banshees against Arthas and put a poisoned arrow in the death knight before Kel'Thuzad's skeletons drove her off.

Out of that ruin came the Forsaken, Sylvanas's free-willed dead, who claimed the land as their own. Their enemies never left it: the Scarlet Crusade holds the old Scarlet Monastery in the north, fanatics who burn and hunt anything not human.

## Agamand Family Crypt

North of Agamand Mills, the Agamand family's dead once rested in this crypt. Captain Dargol, a minion of the Scourge, took the place for his own and woke every corpse and spirit within it. Now dust hangs thick in the dark passages, and the vengeful dead strike at anyone who enters unbidden.

## Agamand Mills

The windmills of Agamand once turned for one of the wealthiest families in Tirisfal. When the Scourge came north, Gregor Agamand refused to flee. He fortified the mills, stocked them against siege, and convinced the family's own workers to stand and fight beside them.

It should have held. It did not, because the betrayal came from inside the walls. Devlin, the youngest Agamand son, turned on his own blood and aided the Scourge, and the garrison could not hold the line against the assaults that followed. In time the plague took the rest of the family anyway, and the Agamands who had died defending their home rose again as mindless dead within it.

The windmills still stand in the highlands of northern Tirisfal, and the family that built them now serves the very host that broke them.

## Balnir Farmstead

The Balnir Farmstead lies northeast of the ruined capital, its fences broken and its stables empty but for wandering Scourge.

The Balnir family once ran this land, breeding the great white horses that the royal house of Lordaeron rode as mounts. On the day Capital City fell, the Scourge came for the farmstead and killed the whole family, leaving only their haunted ground behind.

The snapdragons once tended in the farm's garden still grow wild there, and word has reached the living that the corruption of the Plaguelands may have crept into them. Scourge that slip past the Bulwark gather in numbers around the ruined farm.

## Brightwater Lake

Brightwater Lake lies northeast of Brill, its waters holding a large island at their center where Gunther's Retreat now stands.

## Brill

Brill was once an ordinary town in the kingdom of Lordaeron, held by the Barov family, with the Agamands keeping a manor of their own within its bounds. Its ordinariness ended the day Kel'Thuzad came to it. Still a living man then, the necromancer chose Brill as the place to first test the plague of undeath, and the success of that infestation was so complete that it emboldened him to press the Lich King into launching the invasion of Lordaeron before its time. The dead of Tirisfal Glades were more than the land could bury properly; the great graveyard outside Brill's walls stands as the answer to that surplus. The town's own defenders could not hold it, and what survivors remained fled to join Prince Arthas Menethil as he hunted the source of the corruption sweeping the north.

Brill did not stay silent. Years later the Forsaken claimed it, and Magistrate Sevren now governs what the plague once took. Old grudges linger here too: Coleman Farthing, once in service to the Agamand family, still seeks their remains, bones given not in mercy but in vengeance for a death he blames on them.

## Brill Town Hall

The Town Hall stands as the most prominent structure in Brill. From its podium, Magistrate Sevren speaks for the town, setting the dead of Tirisfal to their tasks. Few such halls anywhere stand in Forsaken hands as this one does, a mark of how firmly Brill belongs to them.

## Cold Hearth Manor

Cold Hearth Manor once earned its name for the warmth of its hearth and the richness of its fields. The Plague of Undeath ended that. Everyone who lived or worked here died in the sickness, and the bountiful ground turned barren, its rows overtaken by mindless Scourge dead who still shamble there.

The Forsaken took the farmhouse for their own, dealing with travelers on the road nearby.

## Crusader Outpost

A scout tower rises in the eastern hills of Tirisfal Glades, held by the Scarlet Crusade. From its height they watch the road south through the glades, ready to flank any force that dares march on the Scarlet Monastery beyond. Captain Vachon commands here.

## Deathknell

Deathknell lies in a narrow, well-guarded valley, and it is here that the newly risen Forsaken first open their eyes to the world they have been given.

Unlike the other holdings of the Banshee Queen's rebel undead, which kept the names they bore as living towns, Deathknell was given a new name entirely, one fitted to what happens there rather than to what once stood there. No one now recalls why.

Survival here is a daily struggle, and gold is the scarcest thing of all. Northwest of the village lies a mine rich in metal, but it has been overrun by spiders, and the dead of Deathknell go out again and again to try to clear it, so the ore beneath might finally be worked. Deathguard Elites stand watch along the valley's edges, the only thing between the fragile new dead and the Scourge and Scarlet Crusade that would end them a second time.

## Faol's Rest

Southwest of the Scarlet Monastery, in eastern Tirisfal Glades, lies a plot of ground once tended by mourners: Faol's Rest, the grave of Archbishop Alonsus Faol.

Faol was revered in life, and his death, some time before the coming of the Scourge, drew mourners to this ground.

The grave he left behind is no longer visited. The mourners who once came are gone, and the plot lies untended, mostly forgotten.

## Gallows' End Tavern

Gallows' End Tavern stands in Brill, its name a grim echo of the town it serves. Beneath its floor, the cellars hold no casks worth mentioning: a captured Scarlet Zealot and a Dwarven Mountaineer are kept there, prisoners of the Forsaken.

## Garren's Haunt

Garren's Haunt was once a bountiful farmstead in the northern Tirisfal Glades, its fields wide and its silos full. The Plague of Undeath ended that. When the Scourge swept through in the Scourging of Lordaeron, Garren and his family were slaughtered where they lived, and the land itself turned gray and lifeless around their ruined home.

What remains standing was claimed by the Rot Hide gnolls, led by Maggot Eye, who took the farmhouse and barn for their own and attacked without hesitation anyone who strayed too close.

The farmstead never recovered. It stands abandoned, its fields empty, its family gone, and only the gnolls remain to answer for who holds it now.

## Gunther's Retreat

On an island in Brightwater Lake stands Gunther's Retreat, claimed by the Forsaken lich Gunther Arcanus and his zombie minions. Off its southwestern shore, on a bare unnamed islet, sits a strange marker known only as Lillith's Dinner Table.

## Night Web's Hollow

Night Web's Hollow is a small valley cut through with an old gold mine, both claimed by spiders. Young ones wander the open hollow while grown Night Web Spiders hold the mine's darker tunnels.

Newly risen Forsaken come here to test what their bodies can still do, cutting down the young spiders outside before daring the mine itself.

In its deepest recess sits the Night Web Matriarch, mother to the swarm above her.

## Nightmare Vale

West of the Scarlet Watchtower in Tirisfal Glades lies a valley gone to ruin. Its trees have withered, and the wildlife that once lived among them has been eaten out by darkhounds and huge vampire bats. Among these hunters flies Ressan the Needler, a rare white bat unlike the rest.

## Ruins of Lordaeron

Above the buried city, the palace of Lordaeron still stands, its halls open to the sky, its gardens gone to ruin. Here the great kingdom of the Alliance died, and here its ghosts have never quite left.

It was Arthas Menethil who ended it. The prince returned home at the head of an army raised against his own people, welcomed through these gates by bells and cheering before anyone understood what he had become. He found his father, King Terenas, waiting in the throne room, and killed him there. Standing over the body, he spoke words that still echo in that room to any who linger: that the kingdom would fall, and from its ashes a new order would rise to shake the world. It was no idle boast. Within days the city and its people were slaughtered, and Lordaeron belonged to the Scourge.

Walk the courtyard still and, it is said, one can hear what the stone remembers: hushed voices, the clang of welcoming bells, a murmur from the throne room that never quite fades. Those who can see beyond the veil say the citizens of Lordaeron still walk there, unaware that their kingdom is gone.

## Scarlet Monastery

Under the Crusade, the Old Monastery became barracks and prison. Inquisitor Whitemane and Renault Mograine made it their seat, and its chapel became a place of judgment as well as prayer.

Before it was a fortress, the Old Monastery was a cathedral of the Church of the Holy Light, filled with libraries where paladins trained. Alexandros Mograine himself spent months there in his youth, praying and studying before he ever bore a blade.

## Scarlet Watch Post

The Scarlet Watch Post stands watch to guard the approach the Scarlet Monastery cannot defend on its own. Captain Melrache commands here, holding the post against any who would strike the monastery's exposed side unseen.

## Shadow Grave

The Shadow Grave stands at the top of the slope within the Deathknell Graves, high among the mountains of Tirisfal. A worn path winds down from its entrance to Deathknell below.

## Solliden Farmstead

The pumpkin fields of Solliden are one of the few patches of ground in Tirisfal where the old life has not been swallowed by plague or undeath. While the country around has fallen to the Scourge and then to the Forsaken who rose from it, the farmers here still work their rows, held safe under the watch of the Scarlet Crusade at the nearby Scarlet Palisade.

That protection has made Solliden an oddity. The farmers trust no one who is not Scarlet, turning away the Forsaken as abominations of the plague that nearly took them, and wary too of the Alliance, whose banners might drag them into a war they have so far escaped by staying small and forgotten.

It is that quiet the Forsaken cannot leave alone. Deathguard Simmer watches Solliden not for what it is now, but for what it might become: peaceful farmers today, armed tenants of the Alliance tomorrow, a foothold in land the Forsaken mean to hold.

## Stillwater Pond

Before the plague, Stillwater Pond was a place for the living to be young. Families from the glades came to picnic on its banks, and children too small to brave the deeper waters of Brightwater Lake learned to swim here instead.

That water is gray now. The fish beneath the surface are only bones still swimming, as if no one told them to stop. Wandering undead and wild beasts pass along the shore, and the darkhound Bayne prowls the water's edge, feeding on whatever is unwary enough to linger.

By the time the Forsaken had begun raising Brill from its ruin, Scarlet warriors had taken to patrolling Stillwater's shore.

## Terrace of Repose

Just north of the Scarlet Monastery lies ground the plague has never touched. Green hedges grow here, and a fountain runs clear rather than stagnant, unlike the rest of Tirisfal Glades. The Scarlet Crusade tends it, keeping this small terrace alive as proof that something clean can still endure amid the blight.

## The Bulwark

The Bulwark stands where Tirisfal Glades meets the Western Plaguelands, the line the Forsaken hold against the Scourge that still masses to the east. Here Forsaken defenders and the Argent Dawn have set aside their differences against that common enemy, watching the border together and sending fighters east to strike at the Lich King's minions.

Unlike the rest of Tirisfal, this stretch does not look like the Glades at all: a plaguemist hangs over it, tinting the sky the same sickly yellow-gray as the Western Plaguelands beyond.

## The North Coast

The North Coast runs where the Tirisfal Glades meet the Great Sea, given over now to murlocs and creatures of the water. Since the Plague of Undeath, the shore has turned dark and cold, and the tide leaves dead sea creatures rotting at the water's edge.

## Venomweb Vale

Venomweb Vale was once green, thick with deer and rabbit, before it became what it is now: a valley overrun with poisonous spiders, led by the rare and deadly Sri'skulk, whose webs have replaced the grass and given the vale its name. Darkhounds prowl the north near the Scarlet Encampment, held by Lieutenant Sanders.

## Whispering Gardens

On the hill south of the Scarlet Monastery, a battle was once fought here between the men who would become the Scarlet Crusade and a host of the Scourge. Many fell on both sides. In the twilight, their spirits still linger, murmuring as faint whispers among the garden's quiet stones.

## Whispering Shore

The Whispering Shore lies in western Tirisfal Glades, north of the Solliden Farmstead. Fishermen once favored its calm water and steady catch.

That bounty is gone. Murlocs have overrun the shore, falling on anyone who comes near, and the water has turned cold and dark.
