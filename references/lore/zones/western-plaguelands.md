# Western Plaguelands

Once these were the fairest farmlands of northern Lordaeron, forested and green, feeding the kingdom's granaries from Andorhal and governed from the hall at Hearthglen. That prosperity became the weapon used to destroy it.

Acting on the Lich King's design, the Cult of the Damned carried his plague energies south in portable cauldrons, hiding them in loyal cultist villages and setting the largest four in Andorhal's own fields. The grain itself was tainted, and it went out across Lordaeron in wagons nobody thought to question. The people who ate their own bread sickened, died, and rose again as the Lich King's slaves. The forests never recovered. The pines stand grey and needled brown under a haze of sickly mist, their trunks swollen with tumorous growths.

What order remains was salvaged after the kingdom's collapse, when the Scarlet Crusade seized Mardenholde Keep at Hearthglen and struck out from there against the undead holding the rest of the land. Amid it all only one place keeps any peace: Uther's Tomb, consecrated to Uther Lightbringer, ground the blight has never touched.

## Caer Darrow

On the island in Darrowmere Lake stood a fortress raised by human hands long ago, holding an ancient high elven runestone. During the Second War the Horde overran the island and sacked the keep, tearing the runestone apart to forge the Altars of Storms, through which Gul'dan bound magic into the ogres of his Horde.

The keep was rebuilt and became the seat of House Barov. As the years turned toward war, the Barovs grew fearful of losing what decades had built them, and struck a bargain with Kel'Thuzad, master of the Cult of the Damned. Beneath the manor the catacombs became Scholomance, a school of necromancy, and the family's own servants were given over as subjects for a plague of undeath. Within half a year the sickness broke loose and killed everyone on the island. Its victims did not simply die: they rose as ghosts, bound to relive their final moments, and still walk the ruined halls of Caer Darrow today.

## Chillwind Camp

Chillwind Camp sits at the southern gate of the Western Plaguelands, where the mountains of Alterac give way to poisoned ground. It is the last place an Alliance traveler can stand and still call the earth clean.

A small contingent of the Argent Dawn holds it, with only a handful of troops and specialists to their name. What the camp lacks in numbers it makes up for in daring: its people wage a guerrilla war against the Scourge stronghold at Andorhal and the plague cauldrons seeding the land with death, relying on mercenaries willing to strike and vanish rather than hold ground they cannot keep.

Promising young Knights of the Silver Hand were sent here by Lord Grayson Shadowbreaker to stand with Commander Ashlam Valorfist against the Scourge in the region.

## Dalson's Tears

Dalson's Orchard once bore fruit for a family that gave it their name. Now the trees stand in the Western Plaguelands under a name that remembers grief instead of harvest: Dalson's Tears.

The Scourge took the farm and turned it into a wound in the earth. At its center they set a great cauldron that has not stopped since, spewing disease into the air, watched over by Cauldron Lord Malvinious and the blighted dead that serve him.

The Dalsons never left. Mrs. Dalson walks the property still, a wandering skeleton picking over ground she once tended as a woman. Her husband did not die beside her. He was shut into the outhouse and left there, and what he became in that locked dark was not a man anymore but a ghoul.

## Darrowmere Lake

The waters of Darrowmere still hold the memory of the Second War, when the lake was crowded with sail. Most of the ships that anchored here flew the colors of the Proudmoore fleet, and before the burning of Quel'Thalas the Grand Admiral sent a number of them across these waters to carry Turalyon, Alleria Windrunner, and their army toward Stratholme. Turalyon weighed sailing on into Quel'Thalas itself, but Alleria warned him that the elves would never let human ships pass up their river, and so the fleet turned toward Stratholme instead. It was in this same war that Zul'jin and his band were pressed back to the lake's shores and the troll warlord was taken captive.

At the center of the lake stands the island town of Caer Darrow, empty now, once held by the House of Barov. Beneath the town's stones lies Scholomance, where the Cult of the Damned works among its undead, a darkness resting under the drowned quiet of the island.

## Felstone Field

Felstone Field once fed the living. That ended when the Scourge came over it and would not let it be a farm again.

At the heart of the field the Scourge set a great cauldron, and it has not stopped its work since. Vapor pours from it without pause, thick with disease. Cauldron Lord Bilemaw stands guard over it, with skeletal sorcerers, skeletal flayers, and slavering ghouls beside him, so nothing living can come near it and walk away clean.

In the northeastern corner the old house still stands, and Janice Felstone still keeps to it, lingering in ground that used to bear her family's crops.

## Gahrron's Withering

The farm at the eastern edge of the Western Plaguelands once belonged to Del Gahrron, and in its better days it was known as Gahrron's Quickening, a place of planting and growth. Nothing grows there now. The Scourge took the fields and set a great cauldron at their center, and the cauldron never stops its work, spewing disease-filled vapor into the air until the ground itself seems to sicken. Cauldron Lord Soulwrath stands guard over it with other officers of the Scourge, and the vapors have called up things that should not walk: Haunting Visions drifting through the ruined rows, Wailing Deaths and Hungering Wraiths answering to no master but their hunger.

## Hearthglen

Hearthglen stands north of Andorhal, its walls closed around a keep, a mage tower, a mill, and a mine, the picture of order in a region gone to ruin. For years it held that order under Tirion Fordring, Lord of Mardenholde, until he gave aid to the orc Eitrigg and was cast out for treason. In time his son Taelan took his place as Lord of Mardenholde.

Hearthglen later sheltered the remnants of the Order of the Silver Hand, who answered Taelan's offer of refuge after the fall of Capital City. From its walls the paladins won victories in the years that followed, until the order was twisted into the Scarlet Crusade and took the town for its own.

## Mardenholde Keep

Mardenholde Keep has stood at the heart of Hearthglen since before the memory of most who walk its halls, its walls unbroken even in the darkest days of the orcish invasion of Lordaeron. For years it was Tirion Fordring's seat, and there, in a council room dominated by a great oak table and a map that rendered Hearthglen down to its smallest road, the paladin met with his advisors on matters of the realm. Charged with treason for aiding the orc Eitrigg, Tirion was excommunicated and cast out, and Mardenholde passed in time to his own son, Taelan.

After the Scourging of Lordaeron, Taelan granted the surviving Knights of the Silver Hand a home in Hearthglen. Word came within these walls that Alexandros Mograine was dead, and suspicion of betrayal split the order: Maxwell Tyrosus led his followers away to found the Argent Dawn, while those who remained took the name Scarlet Crusade, and Mardenholde became their stronghold, with Taelan raised to Highlord.

## Northridge Lumber Camp

Northridge Lumber Camp lies in the northern Western Plaguelands near Hearthglen, its hillsides stripped bare by the Scarlet Crusade's timber cutting. The Crusade works the mill for its lumber, a supply both Alliance and Horde have reason to see spoiled.

Before the Third War, the mill belonged to the family of Nathaniel Dumah, who worked the land until war and plague drove them out. Mickey Levine's family also labored here once.

## Ruins of Andorhal

Andorhal lies in ruin at the heart of the Western Plaguelands, its houses broken and spread across open ground. What remains is held by Araj the Summoner, a lich of considerable power, and the dead that serve him -- ghouls dragging themselves between the fallen stones, skeletons standing sentinel where guards once did, banshees whose cries carry over the ruins, and abominations lumbering through the wreckage of homes.

Araj remains at the center of it all, presiding over a city that never rose again.

## Scholomance

On a rise above the abandoned city of Caer Darrow stands the manor that was once the seat of House Barov, a family whose reach ran from Caer Darrow to Brill, to Southshore, to Tarren Mill. For decades they ruled in comfort, but as the years drew toward war the Barovs grew bitter and grasping, unwilling to let death end what they had built. So they struck a bargain with Kel'Thuzad, master of the Cult of the Damned, to hold their fortune and their land beyond the span of mortal life.

Within six months the keep had rotted into something else entirely. Acolytes of the Cult studied the necromancer's art while the family's own servants were given over as living experiments for a plague of undeath. Before Uther the Lightbringer could pay a scheduled visit to the manor, a sickness bred in its halls was loosed upon Caer Darrow itself. It left no one alive, and bound its victims to wander afterward as ghosts.

The Barovs themselves rose again as undead, still bound to the halls they had sold away. Now the mortal necromancer Darkmaster Gandling serves as headmaster, having succeeded Araj the Summoner, who himself followed Kel'Thuzad.

## Sorrow Hill

Sorrow Hill served Andorhal in gentler days, a place for the town's dead, rich and poor alike, laid beneath crypts and simple stones. That peace ended when the Plague of Undeath came. As the Scourge threw themselves against Andorhal again and again, the hill filled faster than it could hold -- hasty, unmarked graves still scar the ground where the defenders fell. In time the survivors stopped burying their dead altogether, too afraid the plague would raise their loved ones against them, and dug a mass grave south of the cemetery to burn the bodies instead.

The town's last act was its saddest. When Arthas struck down Lord Uther, the remaining people of Andorhal raised a great tomb atop the hill and laid the paladin's ruined body within it before abandoning their home for good. Some say the hill takes its name from that grave alone.

The Plague Cauldrons nearby did not spare the hill's own dead. Soldiers and townsfolk rose from their plots, standing guard over the graves they once filled.

## The Bulwark

The Bulwark stands where Tirisfal Glades meets the Western Plaguelands, the line the Forsaken hold against the Scourge that still masses to the east. Here Forsaken defenders and the Argent Dawn have set aside their differences against that common enemy, watching the border together and sending fighters east to strike at the Lich King's minions.

Unlike the rest of Tirisfal, this stretch does not look like the Glades at all. A plaguemist hangs over it, tinting the sky the same sickly yellow-gray as the Western Plaguelands beyond.

## The Weeping Cave

The Weeping Cave lies west of the Thondroril River, its hollows thick with bog beasts and oozes bred of the plagued land. Rotting behemoths and decaying horrors shamble through the dark. Deeper still, an elemental called the Husk is said to rarely surface, a strange and unsettling thing to glimpse.

## The Writhing Haunt

The Writhing Haunt was once farmland, tended for the sustenance of the living. That purpose is gone. The Scourge took the fields and set at their center a great cauldron, which has not stopped its work since: a constant spew of disease-filled vapor rolling out across the furrows where crops once grew.

The cauldron is not left unguarded. Cauldron Lord Razarch stands watch over it, with Rotting Ghouls, Freezing Ghouls, and Fetid Zombies moving around him, bound to the place as its wardens.

## Thondroril River

The Thondroril River divides the Western and Eastern Plaguelands. It rises far north near the Terrorweb Tunnel and runs south until it empties into Darrowmere Lake, continuing beyond as the Darrowmere River.

Its banks belong to the blight. Maggots and vampire bats crowd the wet ground, plaguebears and spiders stalk the shallows, and snapjaws lurk where the current slows.

## Uther's Tomb

Uther's Tomb stands at Sorrow Hill, the one place in the Western Plaguelands where the Light still holds against the dying of the land.

Uther the Lightbringer once held Andorhal against the Scourge, standing beside Sage Truthbearer and Ballador the Bright with the Knights of the Silver Hand at his back. It was Arthas Menethil who ended that stand, bringing his undead army against the city and cutting down Truthbearer, Ballador, and Uther himself.

What Arthas left behind, Uther's own soldiers gathered up. They carried his body south of Andorhal's ruins and raised a tomb over it. His headstone shows him as he was in life, a tome of the Light in one hand and his warhammer in the other, with words cut into the marker stone before it in the common tongue.

High Priest Thel'danis keeps the tomb still, its caretaker and guardian. The grave is sacred to those who follow the Light, one of the few places in the Plaguelands where such devotion still means something.
