# The Barrens

The dust here was not always dust. Once this was forest, tended by kaldorei who raised the outpost of Then'Ralore, and it is said the demigod Lo'Gosh stood here against the Burning Legion in the War of the Ancients. The Great Sundering ended all that, and the green valley became the sun-scorched plain it is now.

For generations the tauren wandered these plains, hunting kodo and thunder lizard, seeking the Earth Mother's wisdom, forever hounded by centaur who hunted them for sport. They fought back but never for love of war; when the horse-men came, they struck their tents and moved on rather than throw lives away. A year of peace, a year of war -- that was tauren life, until Thrall's orcs and the Darkspear trolls landed and marched with the Bloodhoof tauren against the centaur. After the Legion's fall at Mount Hyjal, Thrall claimed the eastern Barrens, drove off the Razormane quilboar, and named that land Durotar, raising Orgrimmar as its capital.

The land holds older griefs too: it is said a man named Dethorin found his love Allurana in another's arms, and his cry tore the axe Killmaim up out of the sand -- the same blade he used to kill her.

## Agama'gor

Agama'gor lies north of the road running west from Camp Taurajo, a warren of thornwood claimed by the Bristleback quilboar. Their dead leave behind shards of dried blood, tokens the exile Mangletooth prizes and trades his own strange favors for.

## Bael Modan

The stone fortress of Bael'dun Keep rises where a Stonespire tauren village once stood. Beneath it, dwarven excavators dig toward an ancient titan complex, a research facility buried since before the tauren themselves walked this ground, its relics still mostly unclaimed in the dark.

The dwarves came with explosives, blasting into land the Stonespire tribe held holy. The digging tore the earth apart, and it tore the tauren apart with it, most of the tribe killed or scattered from lands they had kept for generations. Their old village now lies torn open by the dwarves' digging machines. Gann Stonespire, who still walks the roads nearby, tried to settle it as one chieftain to another, speaking rather than fighting. The dwarves would not yield the dig. He turned from diplomacy to war, seeking any strength he could find to drive them out.

## Bael'dun Keep

Bael'dun Keep stands above the digsite at Bael Modan in the southern Barrens. General Twinbraid commands it, holding the keep and its dwarves against the tauren and orcs of the plains around it.

## Blackthorn Ridge

Thorns crowd the valley north of Razorfen Kraul, held by Razormane quilboar. On the ridge dwells Brine, a shaman. Below her camp lies a pool of water, clear and pure amid the thorns.

## Boulder Lode Mine

The Boulder Lode Mine lies in the Barrens, its tunnels shored with orcish timber and hung with Horde banners, faded relics of whoever first drove these shafts into the rock. That claim is gone now. The Venture Company holds the mine, goblins swarming the galleries where orcs once worked.

Word reached Wharfmaster Dizzywig that one of the miners there had broken into a seam and pulled free a giant emerald. The wharfmaster wanted it badly enough to promise a share of the profit to anyone willing to go in and take it from the Venture Company's hands.

## Bramblescar

The Razormane quilboar hold Bramblescar. Across the road at Agama'gor sit the Bristleback, and between the two tribes there is only war. Wander too close to the Razormane lair and the quilboar will not wait to be provoked.

## Camp Taurajo

Camp Taurajo stands in the Barrens, near the mountain pass leading into Mulgore. It offers rest and provisions to those traveling from Bloodhoof Village before they press on toward the tauren homeland.

## Dreadmist Den

A dark red mist hangs forever over Dreadmist Peak, cloaking it in twilight. Within, Burning Blade cultists hold Dreadmist Den, guarding the Demon Seed, an artifact they prize above all else. The mist itself seems to shield their demon-worship from outside eyes.

## Dreadmist Peak

Northwest of the Crossroads, Dreadmist Peak rises in the Barrens, its summit choked in red fog - residuum of an ancient demonic curse. Burning Blade cultists have made their home atop it, guarding the Demon Seed within the Dreadmist Den below.

## Far Watch Post

Far Watch Post sits just west of the bridge linking Durotar to the Barrens, little more than a watch tower, an orc burrow, and a line of catapults, with a wolf pen and its two peons a short way south. It is not a comfortable posting, but a necessary one, guarding the caravans bound for Durotar and holding the line against Alliance and quilboar alike.

## Field of Giants

The Field of Giants sprawls across the southern Barrens, wide and open, where herds of thunder lizards and kodo move together in numbers few other lands can match.

The Gold Road runs its southern leg through these plains, less safe here than in the north. Watchtowers once stood along it to guard passing caravans; they stand empty now, abandoned to the wind and the grass.

The Horde has not given up this ground, though its hold on it is thin. On a ridge along the Field's southern edge lives the shaman Brine, apart from any camp or crossroads. Not far off walks Gann Stonespire, nursing an old grievance -- his tribe was driven from its village when dwarves came digging into the earth for titan ruins on land beside their home. That dig became Bael Modan, and Gann has not forgotten what it cost his people.

## Fray Island

Fray Island lies small and dry off the eastern shore of the Barrens.

The island serves as a training ground for warriors of every race and creed, gathered there under Klannoc Macleod.

## Gold Road

The Gold Road stretches between Mor'shan Rampart and the Great Divide, its southern stretch known separately as the Southern Gold Road beyond the bridge that marks the crossing.

## Grol'dom Farm

Grol'dom Farm is one of the pig farms that took root in the years after Orgrimmar was founded, its pens standing under constant raids from the Razormane Pillagers.

## Lushwater Oasis

Water gathers where the Great Divide falls away, and wild beasts come down through the trees to drink it. Kolkar centaur wait among the roots for them, hunting the animals drawn to the pools.

Beneath the oasis lies the entrance to the Wailing Caverns, a place spoken of as accursed. What stirs below does not surface, but its name hangs over the water still.

The fish here are unlike any found elsewhere in the Barrens.

## Mor'shan Base Camp

Mor'shan Base Camp stands south of the border with Ashenvale, near the Mor'shan Rampart, one of the larger Horde holdings in the Barrens. The Warsong Outriders make their base here, watching the road that leads north into the contested forest beyond.

## Raptor Grounds

The Sunscale raptors that fill this small valley are unlike any others in the Barrens. They show an unnatural cunning, building crude huts and fashioning tools with claws that should know nothing of craft.

Stranger still is their hunger for anything bright or unfamiliar, which has driven them to raid nearby Horde settlements, dragging off whatever catches their eye.

## Ratchet

Ratchet sits on the eastern shore of the Barrens, a goblin harbor town doing brisk trade with Booty Bay and Baron Revilgaz across the sea. Gazlowe founded it with the coin Thrall paid him for raising Orgrimmar, and it filled out with smugglers and everyone else respectable places had turned away.

That indifference nearly cost it dearly. The Burning Blade cult worked quietly to set Orgrimmar and Theramore against one another, arranging shipping incidents near Ratchet's harbor that neither side understood as anything but provocation. Orc and human sailors who had shared its docks in peace began brawling in its streets, and when the bruiser Muzzlecrank arrested the captains Klatt and Joq, Northwatch Hold took it as proof the orcs were massing for war. Ratchet became the staging ground for a Horde army readying to march against them. Only when the Burning Blade's hand in the affair came to light did Thrall call the fight off before it began.

## Shrine of the Fallen Warrior

A worn trail climbs a hill west of the Crossroads, ending at a shrine to a fallen warrior. No name is carved there, no banner marks it. The hill keeps its silence, and the path up it is the only sign anyone still remembers.

## Southern Gold Road

The Southern Gold Road takes its name from the caravans that travel it. It runs from the Great Lift at the southern edge of the Barrens north to the bridge joining the Barrens' two halves, where it becomes simply the Gold Road.

Traders come this way from far off: from Gadgetzan in Tanaris, from Auberdine in Darkshore, from Nighthaven to the north. All follow the same road, drawn by the same trade that gave it its name.

## Southfury River

The Southfury rises high on Mount Hyjal, spilling from a mountain lake in a fall that drops thunderously into the lowlands. From there it runs wide and swift, cutting the land in two: it marks where Ashenvale gives way to Azshara, and further south where the Barrens ends and Durotar begins. At its mouth the river throws itself over a last cascade into the Great Sea, just northeast of the goblin port of Ratchet.

Three bridges cross its length, one linking Azshara to Ashenvale, one carrying travelers into Orgrimmar, and one joining Durotar to the Barrens. Just north of the westernmost of these, a single stretch of water touches all four lands at once, the only place where Ashenvale, Azshara, Durotar, and the Barrens meet.

## The Crossroads

The Crossroads sits where the roads meet: the north-south track down from the Gold Road, and the long east-west way that runs from Ratchet out to the Stonetalon Mountains. It is a place made by its crossing rather than by anything built there -- few lodgings, no great walls, just tents and stalls and traders shouting over one another to sell what they have.

Past the edge of the settlement lies a burial ground, plain and quiet against the noise of the trade square behind it. It is here that the Horde comes to pay respects to its dead, much as Sentinel Hill serves the same purpose for the Alliance.

## The Dry Hills

The Dry Hills rise green against the dust of the Barrens, foothills climbing toward Stonetalon. The Witchwing harpies hold this ground, and they have grown bold, falling on caravans along the northern Gold Road before vanishing back into the green.

## The Forgotten Pools

The Forgotten Pools lie north of the western road out of the Crossroads, the northernmost of the oases scattered through the north of the Barrens. Their shores stay green, ringed with Laden Mushrooms found nowhere else, and their waters hold deviate fish unlike any caught elsewhere in the Barrens.

Kolkar centaurs range the water's edge alongside the turtles and wildlife that shelter there.

## The Merchant Coast

The Merchant Coast takes its name from the trade that runs through it, thrifty and constant, in the hands of the goblins of Ratchet.

The southern coast belongs to the Southsea Freebooters. Among them is Baron Longshore, who once stole valuable cargo of Gazlowe's and buried it before he could be caught, leaving the fortune lost somewhere along the shore.

## The Mor'shan Rampart

The Mor'shan Rampart stands where the Gold Road runs north out of the Barrens, raised as a wall against the Alliance push out of Ashenvale. It is a rough thing, built for war: earthworks and watch towers meant to hold a line against forces pressing down from the north.

## The Sludge Fen

Sludge Fen was not always a wound on the land. Once it held a pool of crystal-blue water, before the Venture Company came and raised an oil rig over it. What is left now is a mire of industrial waste, worked under the eye of a goblin supervisor who answers for the operation to the Company.

The rig has drawn rivals as much as it has fouled the water. The Tinkers' Union in Ratchet, pressed by Director Riddlevox to match the Venture Company's newer shredders, gave up trying to out-build them and chose instead to steal the design outright. The goblin Sputtervalve sent Wizzlecrank to seize one of the machines from the fen and haul it back to Ratchet. Wizzlecrank managed only to climb into the shredder before he found he had no key to move it, and sat trapped in enemy ground until rescue came and the machine could be walked out under the noses of the Company's workers.

## The Stagnant Oasis

The Stagnant Oasis is a patch of still, green water between the Merchant Coast and the Gold Road. Kolkar centaur camp along its fringes, their presence and vitality said to come from the stagnant waters themselves. The kodo that wander here are weakened and solitary, unlike the great herds of the Field of Giants to the south, and far easier prey. Deviate fish swim beneath the surface, prized when cooked, while oasis snapjaws lurk in the murk alongside them.

## The Tidus Stair

The Tidus Stair rises above the Merchant Coast as a series of broad stone terraces, stacked like a giant's staircase looking out over the water. Islen Waterseer and Mahren Skyseer, two tauren, keep to these ledges, casting their lines into the surf and speaking with the spirits that dwell in the water below.

## Thorn Hill

Thorn Hill sits on the north face of a mountain top between the Crossroads and Durotar, its slopes thick with quilboar of the Razormane tribe. From this height they have struck at Horde caravans passing through the region, drawing the anger of those who guard the roads below.

## Wailing Caverns

The Wailing Caverns take their name from the sound of the place itself: steam bursting through fissures in the rock, carried through the dark in long, mournful cries.

It was the night elf druid Naralex who found them, and who gave them their purpose. Beneath the savannah ran waterways that surfaced elsewhere as the scattered oases of the Barrens, and Naralex believed that if he joined those waters to the Emerald Dream itself, he could pour that green vitality back into the parched land above. He led his disciples down into the caverns and settled into meditation, opening himself as a channel between the Dream and the waking world.

The Emerald Nightmare took him instead. Naralex could not wake. Through his open, sleeping body the Nightmare's corruption poured into the caverns, tainting the waters, twisting the wildlife into strange deviate forms, and driving most of his disciples to madness -- those who remained became the Druids of the Fang.

Naralex sleeps there still, and the Druids of the Fang hold the caverns against any who would wake him.
