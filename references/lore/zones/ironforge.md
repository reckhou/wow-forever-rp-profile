# Ironforge

Ironforge is the capital city of the dwarves, carved into the depths of Ironforge Mountain in northern Dun Morogh, ancestral home of the dwarves and one of the oldest cities on Azeroth. It is the capital of Khaz Modan, ruled by King Magni Bronzebeard of the Bronzebeard clan.

An expansive underground city of craftsmen, explorers, miners, and warriors, famous for its industry and as an Alliance center of commerce, Ironforge is equal parts fortress and capital, a testament to the dwarves' strength and resilience. The city consists of concentric rings, smaller levels deeper underground, all radiating out from the namesake Great Forge at its center. It is full of small passageways, shops built into the rock walls, and cavernous rooms. The feel of the place is bustling, rowdy, and industrial, yet cozy; fires roar in the hearths of the inns and shops, and much dwarven laughing and frivolity is to be heard. Unlike Stormwind and Darnassus, the city is a massive cavern carved into the earth by the dwarves themselves; the ceiling and floor are both hard stone.

## Deeprun Tram

King Magni Bronzebeard did not build the Deeprun Tram out of comfort. In the years after the Second War, as Stormwind labored to rebuild itself from ruin, Magni grew impatient with how slowly Ironforge's aid reached his human allies, and uneasy at how slowly dwarven soldiers could reach them should war come again. He turned to High Tinker Gelbin Mekkatorque, Azeroth's most esteemed engineer, and set him to the problem. Mekkatorque's answer was a railway driven beneath the earth itself, linking the two capitals directly.

The builders met a subterranean lake beneath the tunnel's path, and did not divert around it. The tram runs through it still, its enclosed tracks passing beneath the water rather than beside it.

Gnomes tend the tram daily, keeping its two sets of triple wagons running fast and smooth between Ironforge and Stormwind, free to all who cross the underground platforms.

## Gates of Ironforge

The Gates of Ironforge rise where the great ramp of Dun Morogh climbs to meet the mountain, visible for miles across the snows before a traveler ever reaches them. They stand as the threshold between the open dwarven lands and Ironforge proper.

Passing through, one comes under the gaze of a huge statue raised to High King Modimus Anvilmar, set to keep watch just inside the entrance, so that none enter the city without passing beneath his eyes first.
